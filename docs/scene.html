Scene

A scene is the open animation with more than one thing in it: rigs, props, cameras, lights, sounds, effects and subtitles. Everything shares one timeline, and one Save keeps it all.

Add to the sceneScene > Add, or + at the right of the Filter strip

Puts another character, a prop, a camera, a light, a sound, an effect, a path or subtitles on the scene's timeline. Each one gets its own row.

  • Rig: another character, picked from the rigs in this place.
  • Prop picked in the Explorer: any part or model without joints. It is greyed while that prop is already in the scene (Props).
  • Camera, from this view: a camera placed where Studio's view is (Camera).
  • Light: a PointLight, SpotLight or SurfaceLight, picked from the lights in this place (Lights).
  • Sound...: an asset id or link, or one of the Sounds in this place (Sound).
  • Screen effects: letterbox bars, a vignette, a fade, camera shake and subtitle lines over the shot (Screen effects).
  • Scene look: the time of day, fog, colour grade, bloom and depth of field (Scene look).
  • Effect picked in the Explorer: a ParticleEmitter, Trail, Beam, Fire, Smoke or Sparkles, or a part holding them (Effects on the timeline).
  • New effect on the picked limb (the menu names the limb): Particles, Trail, Beam, one of the Framez starting effects or one of My effects.
  • Object picked in the Explorer: any other object (Key any property).
  • Path (click points in the view): a path on the ground (Paths).
  • Clip block for the open rig: a saved clip played as a block (Clip blocks).
  • Walk a path: the open rig walks a path. It is greyed until a path is drawn (Walk a path).
  • Subtitles: a row of timed dialogue lines (Subtitles).

anything that moves with the character: another character, a weapon on a table, a door, the camera.

Posing another rigclick its limb or its row, or Scene > Pose this rig

Chooses which rig of the scene you pose. The posed rig's row is open with a "posing" tag, and the handles, IK, the Inspector, Curves and every Pose tool work on it. The other rigs fold to one lane of diamonds and play their own keys. Click any of their limbs in the viewport to pose that rig, with the clicked limb picked.

Pose this rig is greyed until the scene has a second rig. Undo goes back through every rig in order, switching to the one each step was made on.

Propsa box row

Moves a part or model through the scene and keys where it rests. Pick the prop (its row, or click it) and move it with Studio's own Move and Rotate (Ctrl+2, Ctrl+4). When it comes to rest it is keyed at the playhead. Its row shows the keys; the Inspector shows its place as numbers.

Parent to... / Follow a partScene > Follow, aim and ease > Follow a part, or right-click a prop's or camera's row > Parent to...

Makes a prop or a camera ride a part from the playhead on: a hand, the head or the torso of a rig, any other part, another prop, a camera, or the place. For limbs, whole rigs and held objects, see Parent to....

  1. Pick the prop or the camera in the list.
  2. Choose Scene > Follow, aim and ease > Follow a part, or right-click its row and choose Parent to...
  3. Pick the part: click it in the view, or choose it from the list.
  • Click a part in the view: any part of a rig, a prop, a camera's body or the place.
  • Keep where it is: the prop or camera stays where it is and rides from there, with no jump. Snap to it: puts it on the part with no offset.
  • World (stays put): lets it go; it stays where it is.
  • Inspector > Parent: shows the frame and the parent at the playhead, Keep or Snap, and a list of switches (frame, parent, Keep or Snap). Click a switch to go to it. Right-click a switch for Keep, Snap, Blend over, Move to the playhead and Delete. The buttons are Parent to... and World here.
  • Parent row: a row under the prop or camera shows a block per switch, named and coloured by its parent (World grey, a lost parent red). Click a block to go there; right-click it for its menu. The events lane names the ride, for example "RightHand on Head".

a cup carried across a cutscene, a camera locked on a character's head, a camera bolted to a car.

A part cannot follow what follows it: Framez refuses with "That part follows the child." When the parent has gone, the prop or camera stays where it was and shows red; pick a new parent.

A prop's lookInspector, a picked prop: Look, Colour

Keys a prop's Transparency, Scale and Colour. Type a value to key it at the playhead, as for a light's numbers. They show as rows under the prop and play in the game.

CameraScene > Add > Camera, from this view; a camera row

Adds a camera to the scene, keyed where Studio's view is. It is drawn as a camera body with its view as lines. Its Inspector page has its place, Field of view (a Roblox Camera's FieldOfView, top to bottom), Look through it, Key camera here and Cut here.

Look through the cameraScene > Look through the camera, the toolbar's camera, the row's cameraC

Shows the shot in the viewport, with the shot's frame drawn and the outside dimmed. Fly Studio's camera and the scene camera moves with it; K keys it. Moving the playhead without keying puts it back on its keys. C again gives Studio its own view back.

framing every shot, and watching the cutscene as players will.

It is greyed until the scene has a camera.

Key the camera hereScene > Key the camera hereK

Saves the scene camera's place and field of view as a key at the playhead.

a push-in: key the camera, move the playhead on a few frames, fly the view closer and key again.

It is greyed until the scene has a camera. The Camera preview has a Key button too.

Cut to the camera hereScene > Cuts > Cut to the camera here

Starts a shot with the picked camera at the playhead: the view cuts to it on this frame. The cut shows as a block on the Shots row.

It is greyed until a camera is picked in the list. A camera's Inspector page has a Cut here button as well.

Camera pathsScene > Follow, aim and ease > Moves; Inspector > Path

Moves a camera or a prop along a smooth curve through its keyed places, turning smoothly as it goes. It moves at an even speed between two keys, and each key's easing shapes that speed.

  • Smooth path: the curve passes through the keys. Straight moves: goes from key to key in straight lines.
  • Even speed: retimes its keys to give one speed from its first key to its last.
  • Tension (Inspector > Path): straightens the curve; 1 gives straight lines.
  • In the view: a picked item draws its path, a dot for each frame and a red marker for each key. Drag a marker to move that key (X, Y or Z keeps it to one axis, Esc cancels). Drag a dot to key it there.

dolly and crane moves, a camera circling a character, a prop thrown along an arc.

Look atScene > Follow, aim and ease > Look at, or right-click a camera or prop > Look at

Turns a camera or a prop to keep a target in the shot from the playhead on. The target is a rig's head, torso, root or a hand, another item, or the part picked in the Explorer. Its own place keys still move it.

  • Frame at: puts the target anywhere in the shot: Left third, Centre, Right third, or X / Y in %.
  • Lag: lets the item trail a fast target and settle, like a camera operator.
  • Blend: turns over that many frames.
  • Offset: aims beside the part.
  • Stop looking here: hands it back to its keys over the same blend.

keeping a running character framed, a tracking shot, a turret that follows its target.

PathsScene > Add > Path (click points in the view); a curve row

Draws a path on the ground that a prop, a camera or a walking rig can follow.

  1. Choose Scene > Add > Path (click points in the view).
  2. Click points on the ground in the view.
  3. Press Enter or double-click to end it. Backspace takes the last point back; Esc cancels.
  • Move a point: pick the path and drag a point. It stays on the ground; Shift drags it up and down.
  • Add or remove a point: Alt+click the curve adds one; Alt+click a point removes it.
  • Its page: Closed loop, Points stay on the ground, Tension, Add points..., Reverse.
Follow a pathScene > Follow, aim and ease > Follow a path, or right-click a camera or prop > Follow a path

Makes a camera or a prop ride a path from start to end, starting at the playhead. It keeps its height over the ground and the way it faced against the path: a sideways dolly stays sideways. New path from here draws a path starting under it.

A bar on its lane shows the ride; drag its ends to change it. Its page: Speed, Frames, From / To %, ease, Turns with the path, Bank, Up, Side, Bake to keys, Stop riding.

a car along a road, a dolly track, a flying camera over a battlefield.

Camera previewView > Windows > Camera preview, or right-click a camera > Preview it

A window that shows the shot while you pose from any angle. It shows the camera the game sees at the playhead, following the cuts, or the one camera you pick at its top. Float it, dock it, or drag it to a second screen.

  • Header: the picker (the camera the game sees, or one camera from the list), the camera and frame, the eye (Clean view), Key and the more menu.
  • Picture: sits in a dark well. Only the guides and the screen effects are drawn over it.
  • Rail: five icon buttons beside the picture set what a drag does: Turn, Orbit, Slide, Dolly and Lens. Point at one to see its name and what its drag does; the picked mode is filled. Drag inside the picture to fly the camera in that mode. The field of view sits under the rail.
  • Key (or K): keys the camera at the playhead.
  • More menu: Guides, Brightness, Copy the place again and Clean view.

posing a character while watching the shot the player will see.

It draws parts, meshes, characters, the sky and the terrain. Shadows, particles, beams and trails show when you look through the camera (C). It is listed in View > Windows in the Scene workspace.

Clean viewCamera preview > the eye in the header, or more menu > Clean view

Hides the header, the rail and the guides: the picture fills the whole Camera preview window.

  1. Click the eye in the header, or choose Clean view in the more menu.
  2. To bring the controls back, move the mouse over the window and click the eye in its top right corner.

The eye in the corner shows only while the mouse is over the window. The setting is remembered.

Clip blocksScene > Add > Clip block for the rig, or right-click the Clips row

Plays a saved clip as a block over your keys from a frame, on a Clips row under the rig. From another rig lists the saves of other rigs. Each block starts where the one before ends, so walks and steps carry on.

  • Move and trim: drag a block's middle to move it, an end to trim it.
  • Loop and speed: Alt+drag the right end to loop it; Ctrl+drag it to change its speed.
  • Crossfade: overlap two blocks.
  • Right-click a block: Loop, Speed, Blend in and out, Line up with the block before, Holds its last pose, Body mask (the parts it plays on), Open the clip, Bake to keys, Remove (Del).
  • Block page: a picked block's values are on the Inspector's first page.

Posing a part inside a block keys it on a Tweaks layer over the block: the clip keeps its motion and takes your change. Outside every block your keys go to the clip itself.

long cutscenes from walks, idles and gestures; combos chained from saved skills.

Saving bakes the blocks into the clip, so games and other editors play what you see. The blocks stay editable in Framez.

Walk a pathScene > Add > Walk a path, or right-click the Clips row

Makes the rig walk or run a path from the playhead. It eases up to its pace and stops at the end, facing along the path and following the ground. R6 feet are planted on footprints.

Its Inspector page has Walk or Run, Pace (studs a second), Ease (frames) and From / To %. The rig holds where it arrives; add a clip block after it (an idle) and that block starts there.

The row is greyed until a path is drawn: Scene > Add > Path.

FramingScene > Framing

Sets the shape of the shot and draws composition guides over it while you look through the camera. The frame, the camera's lines, the screen effects and the playblast of the scene camera all take the shape.

  • Shape: 16:9 (the game, video), 9:16 (vertical: shorts), 2.39:1 (cinema wide), 1.85:1 (cinema), 4:3, 1:1 (square) or The screen's own.
  • Show the frame: draws the shot's frame.
  • Thirds and Safe areas: guides over the shot.
  • More guides: Grid, Centre, Diagonals, Golden ratio, Golden spiral, Triangles, Your image and Roblox's top bar. Turn the spiral moves the Golden spiral's eye to the next corner.
  • Guide sets: Save these guides... keeps the guides that are on as a set. Pick a set later to turn the same guides on again, or delete it.
  • Guide settings...: grid size, safe area %, line width (1 to 6), colour and opacity, the spiral's eye, Triangles rising or falling, and Your image (an asset id, shown Fit, Fill or Stretch at the guides' opacity).

vertical shorts, a cinema-wide cutscene, keeping a face on a third, keeping text inside the safe area.

Shots row (cuts)above the cameras, with two or more

Shows one block per shot with its name and camera. A second camera added at a frame cuts in there. Drag where a block starts to move the cut.

Right-click a block:

  • Play this shot: loops its frames.
  • Playblast this shot...: films it through the scene camera.
  • Rename shot..., Colour and Camera: change the block.
  • Take thumbnails: puts each shot's first frame on its block.
  • Remove this cut: takes the cut out.
LightsScene > Add > Light; a bulb row: Brightness, Colour, Range

Keys a light's Brightness, Colour and Range. Type a value on the light's Inspector page and it is keyed at the playhead; values ease between keys like any key.

To move the light, add the part it is in as a prop.

SoundScene > Add > Sound...; a speaker row with a block

Puts a sound on the timeline. The block starts where the sound starts and shows its loudness. It plays with the animation, and a slice plays while scrubbing (Sound while scrubbing).

  • Move: drag the block. It snaps to keys and events.
  • Trim: drag its ends.
  • Fade: drag the white handle at a top corner inward to fade in or out (Fades and speed).
  • Speed: Alt+drag its right end.
  • Inspector page: Volume, trim, fades and speed.

The sound must have permission to play in this place, as in the game.

Fades and speedInspector > a picked sound

Fades a sound in and out and changes how fast it plays. Put in the game, the scene plays the same fades and speed.

  • Fade in and Fade out: seconds from each end of the block.
  • Shape: Linear or Smooth.
  • Speed: 25 to 400 %.
  • Keep pitch: from 50 to 200 %.
Screen effectsScene > Add > Screen effects; a film row

Adds letterbox bars, a vignette, a fade to any colour, camera shake and subtitle lines over the shot. Each is keyed like a light's values: type it on the row's Inspector page and it is keyed at the playhead.

  • Subtitle line: shows from its key until the next one. An empty key ends it. The Inspector's Subtitle style sets its font, size, colour, outline, place, width and alignment. For timed dialogue with looks and motion, use Subtitles.
  • Where it shows: while looking through the camera (C), and in the game when the scene is put there.

cutscenes: a cinematic letterbox, a fade to black between shots, a white flash on a hit, a shake on an impact, spoken lines.

SubtitlesScene > Add > Subtitles; a Subtitles row with a CC mark

Puts timed dialogue lines over the shot, one block per line on a Subtitles row, coloured by who says it. The lines show while looking through the camera and in the game. The row appears in the Scene workspace.

  1. Choose Scene > Add > Subtitles. With no camera in the scene, Framez adds one from the view and looks through it. From another workspace, it goes to Scene.
  2. Double-click the Subtitles row to add a line at that frame.
  3. Drag a block's middle to move it. Drag an end to trim it; near the playhead, the end snaps to it.
  4. Open the Inspector's subtitle page to write the line and change its look (The subtitle page).
  • Colours: each speaker gets a colour, in the order they first speak.
  • Right-click a block: Look, Split at the playhead, Subtitle lines (opens the Subtitle lines window) and Delete (Del).
  • A new line: copies its speaker's last look and moves, or the last line's when the speaker is new.
  • Undo and redo: work on the text and the speaker.

dialogue between two speakers with portraits, short-video captions with the said word on a pill, a shouted line, a chapter title.

They show while looking through the camera (C), and Scene > Put in the game... takes them with the scene.

The subtitle pageInspector > subtitle

Writes a subtitle line and sets how it looks, moves and reads word by word. A strip of parts at the top shows a green dot when a part holds the line's own values. A picked limb, key, block or scene item takes the Inspector before this page.

  • Text: the line, Speaker, Look, Starts, Length and At a time. At a time shows a long line as pages of that many words, each timed by the length of its words. A page also ends at a full stop, an exclamation mark or a question mark.
  • Look: font (five kinds, each shown in its own face), weight, size, Fill or Gradient to (a swatch from the palette, plus a hex value), letter gap, italic, capitals, outline, shadow, glow and box. Place sets Top, Middle or Bottom, with Margin (Offset in the middle, up positive), Across, Width and Align. It also holds the speaker's name and a portrait (an image's asset id, its side, shape and size).
  • Motion: In, During and Out, each a list of moves. Add a move opens the move box; the trash icon on a move removes it. Start from fills a part in.
  • Words: a chip for each word, with a dot on a chip that has its own look. A word can have its own fill, weight, size, shake, a pill behind it and italic.

The move box edits one move. A move changes the line as a whole, word by word or letter by letter: fade, move, size, turn, colour, reveal or type on. It has From X and From Y for a move, From size, From angle and From colour. Overlap and Order (first, last, centre, random) show only for word by word and letter by letter moves; Type on has no easing. Moves under During keep running while the line shows: Wave, Shake, Pulse, Colour and Said word (Pill, Lit, So far, a colour and a pop).

Starting looks: pick one in the Look row of Text and it fills every value in: Film, Captions, Karaoke, Title, Shout, Whisper, Lower third or Typewriter. Change any value and the row reads Custom. Save this look as mine keeps your own in My looks, in every place. A line starts with the Film look: white SemiBold with a soft shadow, 7 % from the bottom.

Fonts include Montserrat, Nunito, Roboto, Source Sans and Titillium, in weights from Light to Heavy.

Subtitle linesView > Windows > Subtitle lines (Scene workspace), or right-click a subtitle block > Subtitle lines

A window that lists every subtitle line of the scene and adds many lines at once from a pasted script or an .srt file. Each row shows the time, the speaker and the line; the picked line is tinted. Click a row to go to that line.

  1. Paste the script into the paste box, one line each as Name: line, or paste the contents of an .srt file.
  2. Press Add the lines.
  • Name: line: the lines are timed from the playhead by reading speed, at least 1 s each and 0.2 s apart.
  • .srt: the lines keep their own times and their tags are dropped.

adding a whole dialogue at once, or bringing in captions from a video editor.

Scene look+ next to Filter > Scene look, or Scene > Add > Scene look

Sets the time of day, fog, colour grade, bloom and depth of field for the shot. There is one per scene. Its Inspector page has five sections:

  • Time and sky: Time of day, Brightness, Exposure.
  • Fog: Density, Haze and colour of the place's Atmosphere; or Fog start, Fog end and Fog colour when the place has no Atmosphere.
  • Colour grade: Brightness, Contrast, Saturation, Tint.
  • Bloom: Intensity, Size, Threshold.
  • Depth of field: Focus distance, In-focus radius, Near blur, Far blur.

Focus on: pick a part in the Explorer and press Picked part; the focus follows it from the camera. Press it with nothing picked to stop following. For a focus pull, pick a new part at a later frame.

Typing a value keys it at the playhead. The place's lighting goes back as it was when the scene closes.

In the game, the look goes with the scene (Scene > Put in the game...). The scene player sets it on the player's screen and puts it back when the scene ends.

Effects on the timeline+ next to Filter, or Scene > Add > Effect picked in the Explorer

Puts particles, trails and beams on the timeline, to fire on the frames you choose. Each effect gets its own row, named with the part that holds it, in every workspace. The row shows On blocks (drag either end to move one) and bursts (a star with how many particles).

  • Effect picked in the Explorer: a ParticleEmitter, Trail, Beam, Fire, Smoke or Sparkles, or a part holding them.
  • New effect on the picked limb: Particles (a burst at the playhead, out of the limb's end), Trail (across the hand or foot, on for 10 frames), Beam (from the limb's end, on for 20 frames), a Framez starting effect or one of My effects.
  • Right-click the row: Emit here..., Turn on here, Turn off here, Place it >, Loop around it, Key a property..., Save as my effect... and Select in the Explorer.

While scrubbing, particles move with the playhead and hold still where it stops. Each frame shows that frame's moment, and stepping one frame moves them one frame. Going back past a burst clears it. A paused trail is drawn where it is at that frame.

To play it in the game, see Effects in the game. A pack's effects are in VFX packs.

Framez starting effectsScene > Add > New effect on limb > Framez starting effects

Makes a ready effect at the picked limb's end: Hit sparks, Impact glow, Shock ring, Dust burst, Smoke puff, Fire burst, Energy burst, Explosion, Slash arc or Debris. It is made in a point at the limb's end, emitting out of it, with a burst at the playhead. When another character is near, it is placed where it hits (Place an effect).

Each is made of ordinary particle emitters: every value is yours to change in Studio's Properties window: colour, size, speed, lifetime, count and texture. Most have several layers, each its own row. Hit sparks has a flash and sparks. Dust burst has dust and rocks. Fire burst has a fireball, flame tongues and embers. Explosion has a flash, a fireball, flame tongues, a ring, smoke, rocks and sparks.

Their textures come with Framez (Framez textures): smoke, dust, explosions and fire as flipbooks, and white sparks, glows, rings, stars, slashes and crackles that take any Color.

a first version of a hit, a landing, a blast or a cast, to shape into your own.

Use Loop around it to change the look while it plays. To play it in the game, see Effects in the game.

My effectsScene > Add > New effect on limb > My effects

Keeps an effect you built and lists it under My effects, ready to add again.

  1. Right-click the effect's row and choose Save as my effect...
  2. Later, pick the limb and choose Scene > Add > New effect on the limb > My effects, then the effect.
  3. Choose Rename or delete... in the same list to rename or remove a saved effect.

The My effects list is greyed until you have saved one.

Framez texturesclick an effect's row > Inspector > Framez texture...

Puts one of Framez's textures on any ParticleEmitter, Beam or Trail.

  • Drawn: Spark, Glow, Shock ring, Impact star, Slash, Crackle, Streak.
  • Rendered in Blender: Smoke, Dust, Explosion, Ground blast, Fire, Debris.
  • Flipbooks come with their grid and how they play: once over each particle's life, as a loop (Fire), at random (Crackle), or one rock for each particle (Debris).
  • Colour: white textures take the emitter's Color.
  • As it was: puts back the image the emitter had when it was added.

A streak (Spark) lies along the particle's flight with Orientation VelocityParallel; a negative Squash makes it longer.

Place an effectright-click the effect's row > Place it, or Inspector > Place

Moves the point an effect fires from, and keys that place. An effect in a point (an Attachment) has a Place row on the timeline. Its keys move, ease and delete like other scene keys. Picking the effect's row picks its point in Studio. Move or turn the point with Studio's Move and Rotate tools and the new place is keyed at the playhead.

Right-click the effect's row > Place it, or use the Inspector's Place section. Each choice places the point at the playhead and keys it:

  • Where it hits: the nearest point of another character's body at that frame.
  • Where I click: the next click in the view.
  • At the limb's end: back at the end of the limb that holds it.
  • Aim at the camera: turns the point: the particles leave towards the camera.
  • Aim along the swing (Along the swing in the Inspector): turns the point the way it travels at that frame.
  • Particles move with the limb (Inspector > Place): on, the particles travel with the limb as it moves; off, they stay where they were emitted.

an impact that must land on the other character's body, and a spray that must face the camera or follow a swing.

An effect with several layers in one point moves as one: its Place keys are on its first row. Closing the animation puts the point back where it was. The Place keys are saved with the clip. To play it in the game, see Effects in the game.

Loop around itright-click the effect's row, or Inspector

Plays the frames around an effect over and over while you change the emitter and watch the result. The loop covers its bursts and On blocks, the time its particles live, and a few frames each side. It sets the play range, as Play only the picked keys does. Change the emitter in Studio's Properties window while it loops. Changes made while it plays are not keyed.

While it loops, the same row and button read Stop looping. Stop looping ends it.

tuning a spark's colour, size, speed or lifetime until it reads right.

VFX packsScene > Add > Effect picked in the Explorer, with a pack's part, attachment or model picked

Plays an effect pack's effects the way their maker built them. A pack effect is made with emit attributes (EmitCount, EmitDelay, EmitDuration). Framez adds it as one effect, and each burst fires every emitter inside it with its own count, delay and length.

  1. Pick the pack's part, attachment or model in the Explorer.
  2. Choose Scene > Add > Effect picked in the Explorer.
  3. To play it in the game, choose Game > In the game > Effects in the game.
  • Scrubbing: follows the effect, and so does the game.
  • Part-Icles: an effect made with Part-Icles plays through its own Part_Icles module in ReplicatedStorage.

Run Effects in the game again on a game that already has it, to bring its FramezEffects script up to date with pack support.

Key any propertyStudio's Properties window, or right-click a row > Key a property...

Keys any property of a scene item with an object: a prop, a light, an effect, or any other object added with Scene > Add > Object picked in the Explorer, GUI objects included. Change a value in Studio's Properties window and it is keyed at the playhead. Or right-click a row and choose Key a property... to pick from the object's properties (Studio's own groups, with search).

Numbers, colours, vectors, sizes, positions and ranges blend between keys with Framez's curves. Switches, choices, text and sequences hold until the next key. Everything goes back as it was when the scene closes.

To play it in the game, see Effects in the game.

Scene keysa prop's, camera's or light's lane; Scene > Follow, aim and ease > Ease the picked keys

Picks, moves, eases and deletes the keys on a scene item's lane. Click a key to pick it (Shift adds), drag to move it, Del deletes the picked ones.

  • Right-click a key: Ease to the next key (Linear, Smooth, Ease out, Ease in, Hold, Overshoot, Bounce) or Delete.
  • Ease the picked keys: Scene > Follow, aim and ease > Ease the picked keys gives the same seven styles to every picked key. It is greyed until keys are picked in the scene's rows.
Saving a sceneFile > SaveCtrl+S

Saves every rig's clip and the scene itself with one Save. Every rig's clip is saved under its own rig, an ordinary save Studio's editor can open too, and the scene itself in ServerStorage.FramezScenes. Opening any of the rigs' clips opens the whole scene.

Put in the game...Scene > Put in the game..., or Game > In the game > Put the scene in the game...

Writes the scene into the game as something players can play: from a button, when a part is touched, when a player joins, on a signal or from your code. A new scene starts on Button with its motion In the scene: a first try needs no code and no upload.

  1. Choose Scene > Put in the game... One dialog opens, titled with the scene's name.
  2. Set how it Starts, who sees it, its motion, its look and how players skip it.
  3. Press Put in the game.
  • Starts: five tiles. Button: the game puts a button for the scene at the bottom right of each player's screen, hidden while a scene plays; its label starts as "Play cutscene". Touch: when a player touches a part; pick the part in the Explorer, then press Use the picked part. Join: when a player joins. Signal: when a server script fires the scene's Start signal (the code is in the tip below). Code: only when your code plays it.
  • Who sees it: the player, or everyone. Also: every time, or once for each player.
  • Skip: Off, press, or hold a key for some seconds. Key is Space by default.
  • Rows: Part touched (Touch), Button (Button), Hold (with Hold) and Key (unless Skip is Off) show only when they apply. Changing Starts or Skip reopens the dialog with the right rows.
  • Motion: In the scene or Uploaded animations. In the scene bakes every rig's motion beside the scene; nothing is uploaded, and it plays in group games and in other people's games. Uploaded animations plays each character's published clip; with a clip that is not published, the dialog offers Carry the motion in the scene instead.
  • Look: The place's look now, or The game's own look. The place's Lighting, Atmosphere, Sky, bloom, depth of field, sun rays and blur are set as the scene starts, under its keyed look, and put back after.
  • Goes in: lists what is read from the scene: rigs and cameras, effects, subtitles, held objects, the look's parts, the README and the Framez copy.

The scene goes into ReplicatedStorage.FramezScenes as a ModuleScript, with FramezScenePlayer beside it. Every start but Code also adds a FramezSceneStart script in ServerScriptService and a FramezScenesClient script for players. A scene with effects adds FramezEffects when the game has none, and one with held objects adds FramezHeld; the dialog names both. A README in ReplicatedStorage.FramezScenes lists the scenes there, how they start, how to play one from code, MarkerReached, signals, motion, and how to change or remove a scene.

Every rig's clip markers go out with the scene. Impact frames, effects and held objects still play with the motion In the scene. With StreamingEnabled, the player streams in the scene's place and rigs first. After it, a message tells how to try it: press Play, then the button; touch the part.

A clip that is not published plays in Studio only: Publish it, then Put in the game again. To start a scene from another server script: FramezSceneStart_NAME.Start:Fire(player). The command is greyed until the scene has a second item.

Playing a scene in the gamethe game script

A cutscene, from a LocalScript:

local Scenes = game.ReplicatedStorage.FramezScenes
local ScenePlayer = require(Scenes.FramezScenePlayer)
local run = ScenePlayer.play(require(Scenes.DUEL), {
	rigs = { Noob = character1, Noob2 = character2 },
	origin = character1.HumanoidRootPart.CFrame,   -- where it plays
})
run.Finished:Wait()   -- the camera is given back

Players skip with the key chosen in Put in the game (a Skip button on phones, B on a gamepad); run.Skipped fires and the camera is given back.

Markers: run.MarkerReached(name, value, rig) gives your code each marker of every rig's clip, whether the scene was put in the game with its motion In the scene or with Uploaded animations. The README beside the scene shows how to listen for it.

A grab between players: the server places them and plays both clips; each client plays the camera:

-- server
ScenePlayer.place(scene, { Noob = attacker, Noob2 = victim }, origin)
ScenePlayer.play(scene, { rigs = { Noob = attacker, Noob2 = victim }, origin = origin, camera = false })
-- each client
ScenePlayer.play(scene, { rigs = {}, origin = origin })

Rigs are matched by the names they had in Framez. The other character's own movement must stop for the scene (anchor its root part).

A scene from the gameFile > Import > A scene from the game...

Brings back a scene that was put in the game, with every rig's clip this place lost. Choose it in File > Import, then open the scene with File > Open. The command is described in A scene from the game....

Scene settingsScene > Scene settings

Two settings for working in a scene.

  • Auto key on Studio move: on, a prop, a camera or an effect's point moved with Studio's tools, or the camera flown while looking through, is keyed once it rests. Off, it waits for K. Ctrl+Z takes the key back and the move with it.
  • Sound while scrubbing: Off, One frame, 80 ms or 150 ms. See Sound while scrubbing.
Sound while scrubbingScene > Scene settings > Sound while scrubbing

Plays a slice of each sound under the playhead while you scrub. The choices are Off, One frame, 80 ms and 150 ms. Each time the frame changes while Framez is not playing (dragging the playhead, a frame key, a click on a key), every sound under the playhead plays that long from there. Holding still is silent.

Delete / Remove from the sceneScene > Delete, or right-click a row
