Copying a Move from a Video

This tutorial keys a character's body from the person in a video, such as game footage, a phone video or a dance, and then cleans the result up. By the end you have a first pass of the move on your rig, with smooth motion and feet on the floor, ready to refine by hand.

You need the Framez Helper, installed as described in Installing the Framez Helper, a video in one of its clip folders, and a rig with a new animation open on it. Tracking follows one person, so the video should show the move clearly.

Bring the Video In

  1. Open the Reference video window. It sits beside the timeline and plays the video locked to it.

    View ‣ Windows ‣ Reference video

  2. Click the clip button at the top of the window and select your video from the list, which shows the videos in your clip folders with the newest first. Without the helper, Image frames from files... in the same list uses a numbered set of PNG or JPG frames as the reference instead.

  3. Find the first frame of the move in the video and click the link button. From then on the video follows the timeline, and the keys show under its ruler. See Link.

  4. Zoom the video until the person fills the white box. Turn the mouse wheel to zoom, drag to pan, and double-click to fit the picture again. Tracking reads what the window shows, so zoom in on the person before you key anything.

  5. Click the figure button to turn on Joints. The tracked skeleton is drawn over the picture, with the left side in blue and the right side in orange, so you can see what the tracker found.

Key the Body

  1. Run Pose from video. It keys the torso, head, arms and legs every few frames from the person in the linked video. The camera's angle does not matter, because the pose is measured from the person's own hips.

    Pose ‣ Generate ‣ Pose from video

  2. Play the result beside the video and compare. Expect a rough first pass, since tracking is noisy and guesses the joints it cannot see.

Clean up the Motion

  1. Run Mocap cleanup. It smooths the motion, puts the feet on the floor wherever one is planted, and plants feet that slide. Choose Keep the travel or In place under Travel, and change the other values in Adjust until the motion reads well.

    Key ‣ Clean up ‣ Mocap cleanup

  2. If a single frame jumps far off the line between its neighbours, run Peak removal. A one-frame spike becomes the average of its neighbours.

    Key ‣ Clean up ‣ Peak removal

  3. If a forearm or hand spins round for a frame, run the Euler filter. It turns a limb that flips half a turn in one frame back until it flips back.

    Key ‣ Clean up ‣ Euler filter

  4. Take out the keys the clip does not need with Reduce keys. A key goes when the keys on either side of it play within the error you allow.

    Key ‣ Clean up ‣ Reduce keys

  5. Fix any limb that is still wrong by hand, frame by frame, with the handles.

Match a Weapon or Hand Path

  1. Mark the weapon tip on the video with Marks. Click the picture where the tip is to place a mark, and the marks draw a path 30 frames each side. Right-click the mark button to mark a hand or a foot instead.

    View ‣ Reference ‣ Mark the weapon tip

  2. Show the rig's own motion path for the same point, and drag its dots until it follows the marks.

    View ‣ Overlays ‣ Motion paths ‣ Show motion paths

  3. To compare the outline directly, lay the picture over the rig with Image plane. Drag its bar to move it, use the slider to fade it, and drag its corner to resize it.

    View ‣ Reference ‣ Image plane

Tracking follows one person and guesses the joints it cannot see. Fast spins filmed side-on need the most fixing by hand.
