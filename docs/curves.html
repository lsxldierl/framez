Get the helper

Curves

The Curves window draws each channel of a limb as a line over time. Change the line and the motion between the keys changes with it. Open it from View > Windows > Curves, or the Curves button on the toolbar.

The graphView > Windows > Curves

Shows the picked limb as six curves: Rotation X, Y and Z (in degrees) and Position X, Y and Z (in studs). Time runs left to right, the value goes up the side, and the playhead is the white line. Keys are the diamonds on the curves.

  1. Open View > Windows > Curves.
  2. Click a limb in the view or the track list. Its name and its six channels appear on the left.
  3. Click a channel on the left to show or hide its curve. Rotation X, Y and Z show to begin with.
  • Wheel: zooms time about the cursor. Ctrl+wheel zooms the values.
  • Right-drag: pans the graph.
  • Fit button (Fit the curves): frames every curve in the window.

finding why a move looks stiff or wobbly, and shaping it by hand.

Picking and dragging keys and handlesthe graph

Click and drag on the graph to change the keys' values, which changes the pose on the rig. A handle is the short line out of a picked key that sets the slope of the curve at that key.

  • Click a key: picks it. Shift+click adds keys to the pick.
  • Click a curve: picks all of its keys, on the limb the curve belongs to.
  • Drag on empty space: draws a box and picks the keys inside it. A plain click on empty space picks nothing.
  • Drag a picked key up or down: changes every picked key's value by the same amount. The rig follows.
  • Drag a handle: changes the slope of the curve at its key. A handle stays on its own side of the key.

A key on a locked limb does not change. The message says which limb is locked.

Frame and value boxesthe Curves window's top bar

With a key picked, two boxes in the top bar show its frame and its value. Type a frame to move the picked keys there. Type a value to set every picked key on that curve to it.

  • Alt+drag a key: moves the picked keys in time, a whole frame at a time. A move is refused where another key is in the way or past the clip.
  • Ctrl+drag a key: snaps its value to whole degrees (rotation) or tenths of a stud (position).

moving a hit to an exact frame, setting an arm's turn to an exact angle.

Smooth and HandlesCurves toolbar

Changes how the curve passes through the picked keys. Smooth makes the picked keys (or every key of the limb when none are picked) smooth curves that never overshoot. Handles picks any of seven types for the picked keys.

  1. Pick keys on a curve, or none to use every key of the limb.
  2. Click Smooth, or open Handles and pick a type. Press V to open the same list.
  • Auto: smooth through the neighbouring keys.
  • Smooth (auto clamped): smooth, flat at a peak or a dip, and never past its keys.
  • Flat: level on both sides of the key.
  • Free: each handle moves on its own.
  • Aligned: the two handles stay in one line.
  • Linear (straight): a straight line to the neighbouring keys.
  • Hold: the value waits until the next key.

Handles and Smooth change the picked keys on the curves you picked them on; each curve of a key has its own type. A span tagged "Overshoots" passes its keys: Smooth fixes it. Edit > Preferences > Settings... > Curves and drawing has Auto handles (Standard or Even acceleration).

BufferCurves toolbar

Keeps a copy of the curves to compare with, and puts it back if the new shape is worse. The copy is drawn dim under the live curves.

  • Snapshot: keeps a copy of the shown curves.
  • Swap with the buffer: puts the copy back and keeps the current curves in the buffer; swap again to go back. Shift+B does the same.
  • Show the buffer: shows or hides the dim copy.
  • Clear the buffer: forgets the copy.

trying a stronger overshoot and going back if it looks worse.

Several limbs at oncethe graph

Limbs that have picked keys on the timeline show their curves beside the current limb's, lighter. Their keys pick and drag the same way.

making an arm and a hand reach their peaks on the same frame.

Clean up and ModifiersCurves toolbar

Two buttons with the same rows as the Key menu. Clean up opens Check animation, smoothing, Remove peaks, the Euler filter, Unbake at contacts and Clean up captured motion. Modifiers opens the picked limb's modifiers.

Speed viewthe Curves | Speed switch in the Curves window

Shows how fast the end of the limb (the hand, the foot or the weapon tip) moves on every frame, as one orange line. The window's title gives the top speed and counts the stops and pops.

  • Pop (a red dot): the speed jumps in one frame. The limb seems to snap or twitch. A pop is a change of more than a third of the top speed in one frame.
  • Dead stop (a red band): the limb stands almost still for 3 frames or more between two moves. The motion looks like it died and restarted.

finding the one frame that makes a swing look jerky.

Check animation lists the same pops and dead stops for every hand, foot, the head, the torso and the weapon, and has a fix for each.

The Curves window's keysEdit > Preferences > Keyboard shortcuts... > Curves

These keys work only while you are in the Curves window. The same key can do something else in the 3D view.

  • A: picks every key on the shown curves.
  • X or Delete: deletes the picked keys, as one undo step.
  • V: opens the handle types.
  • Home: frames every curve.
  • F or Num .: frames the picked keys.
  • Shift+B: swaps with the buffer.

Change them in Keyboard shortcuts, in the Curves group. Changing one does not take a key from the view.
