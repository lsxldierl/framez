Curve Editor

The Curve editor draws each channel of a limb as a line over time. Change the line and the motion between the keys changes with it. It is for finding why a move looks stiff or wobbly, and for shaping the motion by hand when an easing style is not enough. The window is available in the Animate workspace.

The Graph

Menu
View ‣ Windows ‣ Curve editor
Workspace
Animate

The graph shows the selected limb as six channels: Rotation X, Y and Z in degrees, and Position X, Y and Z in studs. Time runs from left to right with the frame numbers along the top, and the value goes up the side. The playhead is a white line, and every key is a diamond on its curve. Where keys lie too close together to tell apart, they are drawn as small light diamonds, and a click selects the key nearest the mouse. Each channel has its own colour, with the rotations in red, green and blue.

The list on the left names the selected limb and its six channels. Clicking a channel shows or hides its curve and fits the view to the curves that are left. The three rotation channels show when the window is first opened. With nothing selected the graph asks you to select a limb.

Rotation curves are drawn without jumps, so a turn that passes 180° continues smoothly instead of leaping to the other side. A key you have not shaped in the editor is drawn the way its easing plays: a Steady key is a straight line, a Hold key waits until the next key, and the others are smooth.

Frames outside the play range are dimmed, as they are on the timeline. See Play Only the Selected Keys.

Options

The toolbar along the top holds these controls, from left to right.

Curves, Speed
The switch at the left of the toolbar. Curves draws the channels, and Speed shows how fast the end of the limb moves. See Speed View.
Smooth
Gives the selected keys, or every key of the limb when none is selected, the handle type Smooth. See Smooth.
Handles
A menu of handle types for the selected keys. See Handles.
Fit the curves
The fit button on the toolbar frames every shown curve over the whole clip. Home does the same.
Clean up, Modifiers
Two menus with the clean-up and curve modifier commands of the Key menu. Modifiers also opens the Sound to curve window. See Clean up and Curve Modifiers and Sound to Curve.
View
A menu with Normalize, Falloff, Falloff shape, Ghost curves, Clear ghost curves and the rows of the buffer. See The View Menu.
Normalize, Falloff
Two tiles that switch these on and off. A tile is filled while it is on. See Normalize and Falloff.
Frame and value boxes
Appear at the right end of the toolbar while a key is selected. See Frame and Value Boxes.
Wheel
Zooms the time axis about the cursor. Holding Ctrl zooms the values instead. While a key is being dragged with Falloff on, the wheel sizes the reach of Falloff instead.
Right-drag
Pans the graph in both directions.

Example

For example, if an arm seems to wobble, switch on only Rotation X, fit the view, and look for a curve that rises and falls between two keys where it should travel steadily one way.

Selecting and Dragging Keys

Workspace
Animate

You change a curve by selecting keys and dragging them. A drag changes the keys' values, and the rig follows as you drag. A handle is the short line that comes out of a selected key, and it sets the slope of the curve as it leaves or arrives at that key. A drag is one undo step.

Options

Click a key
Selects it. Holding Shift adds keys to the selection.
Click a curve
Selects every key on that curve, on the limb the curve belongs to.
Drag on empty space
Draws a box and selects the keys inside it, in place of the earlier selection. A plain click on empty space selects nothing.
Shift and drag on empty space
Adds the keys inside the box to the selection.
Ctrl and drag on empty space
Takes the keys inside the box out of the selection.
Ctrl and right-click a curve
Keys the limb of that curve at the frame under the mouse, on the curve, so the motion does not change. The new key is selected on that curve. When the limb already has a key on that frame, that key is selected and nothing is added. The click has to be close to a curve, and a locked limb is refused. It is one undo step.
Drag a selected key up or down
Changes the value of every selected key by the same amount. With Falloff on, the other keys of the same curves move as well, by less the farther they are. See Falloff.
Drag a handle
Changes the slope of the curve at its key. A handle stays on its own side of the key, so time only runs forward. Dragging a handle on a key that is not Free or Aligned makes it Aligned, so the two handles stay in one line.

Notes

A key on a locked limb does not change, and the message names the limb that is locked. A key you change here plays as the graph shows it, with each channel on its own curve between the keys. When the clip is saved, a span shaped this way is written as a pose on every frame, as an eased span is.

Frame and Value Boxes

Workspace
Animate

When a key is selected, two boxes appear in the toolbar with its frame and its value. Type a frame to move the selected keys there. Type a value to set every selected key on that curve to it, in degrees for a rotation or studs for a position. Both are exact, which a drag is not. With Normalize on, the value box still shows and takes the real value.

When there is room, the title in the toolbar names the limb and the channel of the selected key.

Options

Alt and drag a key
Moves the selected keys in time, one whole frame at a time. A move is refused where another key is in the way, and the keys cannot be moved past the ends of the clip. A key that comes close to a beat bookmark lands on it. See Sound to Curve.
Ctrl and drag a key
Snaps its value to whole degrees for a rotation, or to tenths of a stud for a position.

Example

For example, to land a hit on an exact frame, select its keys, type the frame in the frame box, and press Enter. To set an arm's turn to exactly 45°, select the key on its rotation curve and type 45 in the value box.

Smooth

Menu
Curve editor toolbar ‣ Smooth
Workspace
Animate

Smooth turns the selected keys into smooth curves that never overshoot their keys. It gives each key the handle type Smooth (auto clamped), which is smooth through the neighbouring keys and flat at a peak or a dip. With no key selected, it smooths every key of the limb.

When a span is drawn with the tag Overshoots, with a number of degrees beside it, the curve passes beyond one of its keys, and Smooth removes that.

Example

For example, if an arm swing made from straight segments shows a corner at every key, select the limb and press Smooth. The corners round off, and the arm keeps reaching each key.

Handles

Menu
Curve editor toolbar ‣ Handles
Shortcut
V
Workspace
Animate

The Handles dropdown sets how the curve passes through the selected keys, choosing from eight handle types. When one key is selected, the dropdown reads Handles with the type of that key's curve. V opens the same list at the cursor. With no key selected, the dropdown changes every key of the limb.

Each channel of a key has its own handle type. A key selected on some of its curves changes on those channels only, so you can make one channel straight and leave the others on the key's easing.

Options

Key easing
The key's own easing style and direction, as set in the Easing menu. Channels you have not changed keep it.
Auto
Smooth through the neighbouring keys. It can overshoot a key.
Smooth (auto clamped)
Smooth, flat at a peak or a dip, and never past its keys.
Flat
Level on both sides of the key.
Free
Each handle moves on its own.
Aligned
The two handles stay in one line.
Linear (straight)
A straight line to the neighbouring keys.
Hold
The value waits at this key until the next key.

Example

For example, give the key at the moment of impact the type Hold on its position channels, so the weapon waits there for a beat before the follow-through starts.

Notes

The Preferences window has a Curves group with Auto handles, which can be Standard or Even acceleration. Standard slopes an automatic handle through the keys on either side of it, and Even acceleration takes the gaps between the keys into account. It applies to every Auto and Smooth key.

The View Menu

Menu
Curve editor toolbar ‣ View
Workspace
Animate

The View menu gathers the commands that change how the curves are drawn and how a drag treats the keys round the one you move. Normalize and Falloff are also on the two tiles beside the menu, and a tick in the menu shows which of them are on.

Options

Normalize
Draws every curve between -1 and 1. See Normalize.
Falloff
Makes a drag move the keys near the selected ones as well. O does the same. See Falloff.
Falloff shape
Opens the list of shapes, Smooth, Sphere, Root, Sharp, Linear and Constant. The shape in use has a mark beside it.
Ghost curves
Keeps a faded copy of the shown curves. Ctrl+G does the same.
Clear ghost curves
Removes every ghost curve. Ctrl+Alt+G does the same. The row is greyed when there are none.
Snapshot, Swap with the buffer, Show the buffer, Clear the buffer
The rows of the buffer. See Buffer and Ghost Curves.

Normalize

Menu
Curve editor toolbar ‣ View ‣ Normalize
Curve editor toolbar ‣ Normalize
Workspace
Animate

Rotation curves are in degrees and position curves are in studs, so on one scale a small move looks flat beside a large turn. Normalize draws every curve between -1 and 1, from the lowest and the highest value among its own keys, so the shapes of all the channels can be read together. The value scale at the side then runs in those units.

Only the drawing changes. Dragging a key or a handle edits the real value, and the value box still shows the real value in degrees or studs. The scale of a curve is worked out from its keys each time the graph is drawn, and it stays fixed while a key is being dragged, so the curve does not change size under the mouse. Turning Normalize on or off fits the view, and Home frames the range from -1 to 1 while it is on.

Example

For example, to see whether a hand's height and an arm's turn peak on the same frame, switch Normalize on, show Rotation X and Position Y, and compare where the two curves top out.

Falloff

Menu
Curve editor toolbar ‣ View ‣ Falloff
Curve editor toolbar ‣ View ‣ Falloff shape
Curve editor toolbar ‣ Falloff
Shortcut
O in the Curve editor
Workspace
Animate

With Falloff on, dragging selected keys up or down moves the other keys of their curves as well. A key moves by a share of the drag that gets smaller the farther the key is, in frames, from the nearest selected key on its curve. The shape sets how the share drops, and the reach is the number of frames over which it drops to nothing. Keys at the reach or beyond do not move. The reach starts at 10 frames.

While a key is being dragged, a circle round it shows the reach, as wide as the reach on either side of the key. Turn the mouse wheel during the drag to make the reach larger or smaller, and the keys follow as the circle changes. Outside a drag the wheel zooms the graph as before. The shape and the reach are kept for the next time you use Falloff, and a whole drag is one undo step.

Falloff changes values only. Moving keys in time with Alt and drag does not use it, and the keys of a locked limb are left alone.

Options

Smooth
Eases from the whole drag at the selected key to nothing at the reach, gently at both ends.
Sphere
Stays close to the whole drag for most of the reach, then drops steeply at its edge.
Root
Falls slowly at first and faster toward the edge of the reach.
Sharp
Drops steeply beside the selected key and tails off slowly, so only the nearest keys follow much.
Linear
Falls in a straight line from the whole drag to nothing.
Constant
Every key inside the reach moves by the whole drag.

Example

For example, select the key at the top of an arm's swing, press O and drag the key up. The keys on either side rise with it, less the farther away they are, so the whole peak changes shape.

Buffer and Ghost Curves

Menu
Curve editor toolbar ‣ View ‣ Snapshot
Curve editor toolbar ‣ View ‣ Swap with the buffer
Curve editor toolbar ‣ View ‣ Show the buffer
Curve editor toolbar ‣ View ‣ Clear the buffer
Curve editor toolbar ‣ View ‣ Ghost curves
Curve editor toolbar ‣ View ‣ Clear ghost curves
Shortcut
Shift+B swaps with the buffer
Ctrl+G keeps ghost curves
Ctrl+Alt+G clears them
Workspace
Animate

The buffer keeps a copy of the shown curves so you can compare them with the changes you make, and put the copy back if the new shape is worse. The copy is drawn dimmed underneath the live curves. It holds the keys of the limbs shown in the graph.

Ghost curves are faded copies of the shown curves that stay in the graph while you go on editing, so you can see where a curve was. They are only drawn. They are never swapped back and they do not change the animation. Up to five are kept, the older the fainter, and making another drops the oldest.

Options

Ghost curves
Keeps a faded copy of every shown curve, with its keys as they are now. When no shown limb has keys, the status line says there are no curves to keep.
Clear ghost curves
Removes every ghost curve. The row is greyed when there are none.
Snapshot
Keeps a copy of the curves as they are now.
Swap with the buffer
Puts the copy back as the live keys and keeps the current ones in the buffer. Swapping again goes back. It is one undo step, and locked limbs are left alone. If the buffer is empty the status line asks you to take a snapshot first.
Show the buffer
Shows or hides the dim copy.
Clear the buffer
Forgets the copy.

Example

For example, take a snapshot, push the overshoot of a swing further, and play it. If it looks worse, swap with the buffer to be back where you started. To keep several earlier shapes in view instead, press Ctrl+G before each change, and the faded copies stay behind the live curve.

Several Limbs

Workspace
Animate

Limbs that have selected keys on the timeline show their curves beside the current limb's, lighter and thinner. Their keys are selected and dragged in the same way as the current limb's, and the toolbar names the current limb with the number of others beside it.

Example

For example, select the keys of an arm and its hand on the timeline and open the Curve editor, to make both reach their peaks on the same frame.

Clean up and Curve Modifiers

Menu
Curve editor toolbar ‣ Clean up
Curve editor toolbar ‣ Modifiers
Workspace
Animate

Two buttons on the toolbar open the same commands as the Key menu, so you can use them without leaving the graph. When a clean-up dialog previews its result, the original curve is drawn faintly under the new one, so you can see what the tool changed.

Options

Clean up
Opens Check animation, Butterworth smooth and Gaussian smooth, Peak removal, the Euler filter, Unbake at contacts and Mocap cleanup.
Modifiers
Opens the curve modifiers of the selected limb, to add modifiers such as noise, a wiggle, cycles or stepped playback to its curves, and to change, turn off or bake the ones it has. The menu ends with Sound to curve..., which opens the window described in Sound to Curve.

Sound to Curve

Menu
Curve editor toolbar ‣ Modifiers ‣ Sound to curve...
Key ‣ Clean up ‣ Curve modifiers ‣ Sound to curve...
Needs
An animation open on a rig
Workspace
Animate

Sound to curve turns the loudness of a sound into keys on one curve, and the loud beats of the sound into bookmarks. Use it to make a part move with music or a voice, and to mark the beats of a song so that keys can be placed on them.

The window reads the sound from the frame it starts on to the end of the clip, measures how loud it is on each frame after the Sound shaping options have been applied, and scales that from 0 for silence to 1 for the loudest moment. With Make on Curve or Both, the curve gets a key on every frame, at Silence where the sound is quiet and at Loudest where it is loudest. These are Generated keys, the small grey keys Framez makes from motion. A key already on one of those frames is replaced, and the other channels of the limb keep the values they had there. With Make on Marks or Both, a bookmark goes on every frame where the loudness rises past the Beats settings, named Beat 1, Beat 2 and so on. Making is one undo step.

Options

The window has the sections Sound, Output, Sound shaping and Beats, with a Make button at the bottom.

Sound
A list of the sounds on the timeline. Choosing one sets Asset id and Starts at from it. With no sound on the timeline the list says so, and you type an id instead. When the window opens with no sound chosen, it takes the first sound of the timeline.
Asset id
The id of the sound, as digits or as rbxassetid:// and digits. Typing an id here chooses that sound in place of the one from the list.
Starts at
The frame of the animation that the sound starts on. The keys and the beats begin there. It starts at 0.
Make
Curve makes the keys, Marks makes the beat bookmarks, and Both makes both.
Curve
The curve that gets the keys. It is the curve of the key selected in the Curve editor or, with none selected, the first curve shown for the limb, and the row names the limb and the channel. With no limb it asks for a key's curve. Marks does not need a curve.
Silence
The value of the curve where the sound is silent, in degrees for a rotation or studs for a position. It starts at 0.
Loudest
The value of the curve where the sound is loudest, in the same unit. It starts at 30.
Lowest
In Hz. Sound below this frequency is left out of the measurement. It starts at 0, which leaves everything in.
Highest
In Hz. Sound above this frequency is left out. It starts at 8000, which is the most it can be.
Attack
In milliseconds. How quickly the loudness follows the sound when it gets louder. At 0 it follows at once. It starts at 5.
Release
In milliseconds. How slowly the loudness follows the sound when it gets quieter. A longer release lets the curve fall away gradually after a hit. It starts at 200.
Threshold
As a percentage. Loudness below this share of the loudest moment counts as silence. It starts at 0, which changes nothing.
Accumulate
The curve only rises. Each rise in loudness is added to it, so it climbs through the sound and ends at Loudest.
Additive
Adds the result to what the curve already does on each frame, in place of replacing it.
Square
The curve takes only two values, Loudest wherever the sound is above the Threshold and Silence everywhere else.
Louder than
As a percentage. A beat is a frame where the loudness rises past this share of its loudest moment. It starts at 50.
Apart
In frames. The fewest frames allowed between one beat and the next. It starts at 8.
Make
Reads the sound and makes what the Make switch says. The status line says Reading the sound while it works, and then how many keys and beats it made. A sound that does not load, or a locked limb, is reported there and nothing is made.

Notes

The beats are measured without Threshold, Accumulate and Square. Beat bookmarks are orange. Making marks again replaces the earlier beats, and the bookmarks you made yourself are kept.

A key dragged on the timeline, on its own or as a group, or moved in the Curve editor with Alt and drag, lands on a beat bookmark when it comes close to one. Bookmarks that are not beats do not pull keys.

Example

For example, to make a character's chest bob with a song, select a key on its Position Y curve, open Sound to curve, choose the song, set Loudest to a small number of studs, set Make to Both and press Make. The chest follows the loudness, and the Beat bookmarks show where the song hits, ready for keys to be dragged onto them.

Speed View

Menu
Curve editor toolbar ‣ Speed
Workspace
Animate

The Speed view shows how fast the end of the limb moves on every frame, as one orange line. The end is the hand, the foot, the head or the weapon tip, or the far end of the part. The toolbar title gives the top speed in studs per second and counts the stops and pops. Two kinds of fault are marked on the line.

Options

Pop
A red dot where the speed changes by more than a third of the top speed in one frame. The limb seems to snap or twitch there.
Dead stop
A red band where the limb stands almost still for three frames or more between two moves. The motion looks as if it died and restarted.

Example

For example, if a sword swing looks jerky, switch to Speed and look for the single red dot on the frame where the blade stutters, then correct the key near it.

See Also

Check Animation lists the same pops and dead stops for every hand, foot, the head, the torso and the weapon, with a fix for each.
