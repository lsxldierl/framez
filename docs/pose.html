Menus/Pose
Get the helper

Pose

Everything for one limb is also on its right-click, in the 3D view or on its name in the track list. Pose > its name lists the same, except Key this limb, which is in Key. An object it holds gets Held by, Put in hand and Let go there.

Rotate / MovePoseR

Chooses what the handles on the picked limb do. Rotate shows rings that turn the limb; Move shows arrows that slide it. Press R to switch. Drag a ring or an arrow to pose the limb; with Auto key on, letting go keys the pose.

  • Shift: hold it while dragging for slow, fine moves.
  • Ctrl: hold it while dragging to snap turns to 5-degree steps and moves to 0.1 stud steps.

Edit > Preferences > Settings..., category Handles and posing, holds Fine and snap (Shift's speed and the two Ctrl steps) and Auto key.

Several limbs at onceShift+click limbs in the 3D view

Poses more than one limb with one drag. Hold Shift and click a limb to add it to the pick, or to take it off again. Picked limbs have an orange outline. A plain click or Esc goes back to one limb.

  • Rings and arrows: the handles stay on the limb picked last. A drag turns or moves every picked limb about its own joint. With World axes on, they all turn and move the same way in the world. One undo step.
  • IK: IK means the arm or leg bends to follow the hand or foot you drag. With several IK hands or feet picked, one drag moves them all.
  • Commands that take every picked limb: Reset limb, Mirror onto, Pin here, Release here, Delete this pin, Carry pose through the hold, Apply capture to the picked limb, Copy world place and Paste world place.
Full bodyPose > Posing

With Full body on, a hand or foot dragged out of its limb's reach pulls the body: the torso leans, turns and shifts, the hips follow, and the hand or foot reaches. Planted feet and pinned limbs stay where they are, and free arms ride the torso. Letting go keys every part that moved, as one undo step.

The Inspector's Full body section sets how the body gives. The values are kept on the rig.

  • Pull (on a hand or foot): how soon the body starts to help. 0 means only when the limb is straight.
  • Stiffness (on the torso): how much the torso resists moving. 100 keeps it still.
  • Hips (on the root): how much the hips resist moving. 100 keeps them still.
  • Planted (on the root): how firmly planted feet and held hands stay. A lower value lets them give a little.

a reach to the floor, a lunge, a grab across the body, a hand pulling the body round. R6, R15 and mapped rigs.

World axesPose > Posing, or the globe on the toolbarCtrl+L

The rings and arrows line up with the world instead of the limb: move a hand straight up or along the ground whatever its tilt. Hold Ctrl while moving for 0.1 stud steps (turns snap in 5-degree steps). With several limbs picked, they all move the same way in the world.

Stand rig on the floorPose > Posing

Moves the rig down or up until its lowest foot touches the floor.

Place rigPose > Posing

Moves or turns the whole rig in the world with Studio's own Move and Rotate tools while the animation is open. Click Place rig again to finish. One Ctrl+Z puts it back.

Joint limits / Limbs stop at the bodyPose > Posing

Joint limits: each joint stops at its range while posing, by rings, arrows or IK. Limbs stop at the body: a limb stops where it meets the torso or another limb; limbs that already touch when the drag starts are left alone. Hold Alt while dragging to pass both for that drag.

The picked limb's range is in the Inspector, section Limits: Limit this joint, then Swing (how far it swings from rest) and Twist min / Twist max, or Bend min / Bend max for a hinge such as an R15 elbow or knee. Use this pose sets the range to reach the pose as it stands; Default goes back to the rig's default. The range is kept on the joint and travels with the rig.

Defaults: R15 elbows and knees bend one way only, wrists, ankles, neck and waist have short ranges. R6 arms twist up to a quarter turn each way, and legs swing up to a third of a turn. A pose already outside its range stays as it is until it is dragged back in.

Spread editsPose > Posing > Spread edits and Spread settings...O

While on, a pose change made with the handles also goes into the keys around the playhead: all of it at the playhead, fading to none at the band's edges. The band shows on the events lane. One Ctrl+Z undoes the edit and the spread.

Spread settings... opens the values:

  • Before and After: the frames the band reaches on each side.
  • Shape: Smooth, Linear, Sharp, Root, Flat top or Drawn. Drawn uses the curve you draw in Spread settings, from the playhead on the left to the edge of the band on the right.
  • Keys in range or Every frame: which keys change. Every frame keys each frame in the band first, for an even fade.

a guard raised at one frame that should carry into the frames around it, a lean added to a whole stretch.

Squash and stretchPose > Posing > Squash and stretch, or Inspector > Squash and stretch

Squashes or stretches a part, or the whole body, and keys the size. Pick a limb, then open Squash and stretch. Choose This part or Whole body. Stretch: below 1 squashes, above 1 stretches along the part's length; Keep volume makes it thinner as it stretches. X, Y and Z set any other shape. Changing a value keys it at the playhead.

  • Key here: keys the size at the playhead.
  • Delete key here: removes the size key at the playhead.
  • Open the settings: shows the Inspector's Squash and stretch section.
  • Squash handles: shows Roblox's resize handles on the picked part. Drag a face of the part and it stretches as you drag; Keep volume squashes the other two sides. Letting go keys it. Esc or the right mouse button puts it back.

A Squash and stretch row appears under the part (Whole body squash under the body). Drag its keys; right-click one for easing or Delete. The limb stays joined. The whole body keeps its feet on the floor in any pose. Save as mine in the section keeps a size and its easing under a name, to key again with one click.

In the game it needs the FramezEffects script: Game > In the game > Effects in the game. It works on any character, R6 and R15. A mesh bone refuses it: bones do not scale.

Reset limbright-click the limb, or Pose > its name

Puts the picked limb back at its rest pose and keys it. With several limbs picked, each one is reset.

  • Turn and move: both go back to rest.
  • Turn only: the turn goes back to rest; where the limb has moved stays.
  • Move only: the move goes back to rest; how the limb has turned stays.
Parent to...right-click a limb, an object, or a prop's or camera's row; Pose > its name; Inspector > Parent

From the playhead a limb, the whole rig, a held object, a prop or a camera rides any part you choose: a part of this rig, of another rig, a prop, a camera or the place. The parent's own animation counts. Keep where it is leaves it in place and Snap to it puts it on the part. World (stays put) lets it go; Its own rig brings a limb back.

  1. Go to the frame where the ride starts.
  2. Right-click the limb, object, prop or camera and choose Parent to....
  3. Choose the parent from the list, or choose Click a part in the view and click any part of a rig, a prop, a camera's body or the place.
  • Click a part in the view: picks any part you click.
  • Its own rig: the limb goes back to its own rig. It is greyed when it is already there.
  • This rig: the holders (Right hand, Left hand, Head, Torso, Right foot, Left foot) and More parts for every other part.
  • Other rigs: each other rig in the scene, with its holders and More parts.
  • Props and cameras: the props and cameras of the scene.
  • World (stays put): it stays where it is in the world from this frame.

Each switch is Keep or Snap, and a limb eases in over Blend frames. The Inspector's Parent section lists the switches. The timeline shows them as named blocks in a row under the limb, and the events lane names the ride (for example RightHand on Head).

a sword passed hand to hand and caught by another character, a hand on a moving car door, one character riding on another's shoulders, a camera bolted to a car.

A part that follows the thing you are parenting is refused with the message "That part follows the child." A parent that has gone leaves the child where it was and shows red; pick it again.

Mirror onto twinright-click the limb, or Pose > its name

Puts this arm's or leg's pose, mirrored, on its twin. The menu names the twin (Mirror onto LeftUpperArm). Only arms and legs have a twin. With several limbs picked, each one goes onto its own twin.

Hideright-click the limb > HideH Ctrl+H Alt+H

Hide in view hides the limb in the 3D view, Show only this hides every other limb, and Show all brings everything back. See Hide in view.

Paste onto this limbright-click the limb

Puts keys copied from one limb onto the picked one. See Edit.

Look at / Point atright-click the limb, or Pose > its name

The head turns to face, or the arm points at, the camera or a part picked in the Explorer. The menu reads Look at on the head and Point at on any other limb. It makes an Aim constraint: its frames, target and influence open in Adjust and stay changeable in the Made window. See Constraints.

eye contact in a cutscene, aiming a cast, pointing at something.

Carry pose through the holdright-click the limb, or Pose > its name

The run of equal keys after the playhead takes this key's new pose.

a changed pose that is held for several keys, when the hold should change with it.

Pivot (Turn about)right-click the limb > PivotAlt+click

Turns the picked limb, object or whole body (the torso) about a point you choose instead of its joint. The rings move to that point. Choose the point in the Pivot list:

  • Its joint: the limb's own joint. This is the normal turn.
  • Its far end, Left heel, Left toe, Right heel, Right toe, Left hand, Right hand, Weapon tip, Floor under the body.
  • Place a point: puts down a red ball. Drag it with Studio's Move tool (Ctrl+2).
  • Alt+click on the body puts the point where the click lands.

Planted feet and the head hold as for any turn, and letting go keys it. Esc, Its joint or picking another limb goes back to the joint. Turn about a point in Pose > Physics spins the whole body about a point with a live angle.

a foot pivoting on its heel or toe, a cartwheel over a planted hand, a body turning round a sword stuck in the ground. R6, R15 and any Motor6D rig.

Edit pivot...right-click the limb > Pivot

Moves the point the picked part turns about, such as a sword's grip, a door's hinge or a creature's joint. The part does not move at rest. Type the move along the part's own axes, use Centre, Far end or Original, or drag the red ball with Studio's Move tool (Ctrl+2). "Keep the motion" changes the numbers and keeps every key looking as it did; "Turn about the new pivot" keeps their numbers. One Ctrl+Z puts it back.

weapons, props and custom rigs whose joints sit in the wrong place.

The box shows the joint's new C0 and C1 for game scripts. A player's own joints (Neck, Shoulders, Hips...) keep Roblox's pivots in the game. Use it on rigs and parts you ship yourself. Other saves of the rig that key that part turn about the new pivot too. A mesh bone refuses it and points to Pivot > Turn about.

Pinright-click the limb > Pin, or Pose > its name > Pin

A pin holds a limb at one place in the world from the playhead while the body moves. A hand or foot is reached by IK (the arm or leg bends to reach it), the head keeps its angle, a prop stays put. The yellow ball in the view and the bar on the events lane show it. Posing a pinned limb moves its pin.

  • Pin here: starts the pin at the playhead. Inside a pin the row reads Move the pin here and moves the pin to where the limb is.
  • Release here: ends the pin at the playhead. The keys lead again.
  • Pin weight: how far toward the pin the limb goes (100 % is all IK, 0 % is all keys). It also sets It holds (Place and angle, Place only, Angle only), Weight over the pin (a curve over its frames), The weight follows (Nothing, The frame, or a joint's turn between None at and Full at) and this pin's own blend in and out.
  • Blend for new pins: how many frames a new pin eases in and out. Choose At once, 2 frames, 4 frames or 8 frames, or type any number from 0 to 120.
  • Bake into keys: turns the limb's pins into ordinary keys.
  • Delete this pin and Delete all pins on this limb.

Drag a pin's bar on the events lane: an end moves that end, the middle moves the whole pin. Pin here, Release here and Delete this pin work on every picked limb. Saving bakes the pins into keys. Opening the save restores the pins.

a planted foot while the hips drop, a hand on a wall or a ledge, a head that keeps looking at one spot, a sword stuck in the ground while the body moves round it.

Hand on object / Other hand on itright-click > Pin

A two-handed grip: the hand stays where it is on the held object from the playhead, wherever the object goes. Hand on object is in a hand's Pin list while the rig holds an object. Other hand on it is in the held object's own Pin list and needs the object held in one hand first. Release here ends it.

a spear swung with both hands, a box carried in two.

Hold the picked object / Put in hand / Let go of this objectright-click the limb, or Pose > its name

Puts an object in a limb's hand, hands it back to its holder, or lets it go.

  • Hold the picked object: the object picked in the Explorer (a part, model, tool or accessory) is joined to the picked limb with the animation open. All of its pieces ride along, and a far object lands in the hand by its handle.
  • Put in hand: the picked object's handle goes back on its holder's grip, keyed here.
  • Let go of this object: the object leaves the rig and stays where it is.

Ctrl+Z undoes each one. Who holds the object from each frame is set in the Inspector's Held by.

A tool sits in the hand the way Roblox holds it (its Grip). For other objects, set the attribute FramezHandle (and FramezTip for the far end) on the held part, in studs, in its own space. Without them, Framez uses the ends of the whole object.

Throw or drop...right-click a held object

From the release frame the object lets go and flies gravity's arc to the floor. Distance goes the way the hand was moving, Height is how far it rises over the release at the top (0 for a plain drop), Spin is in whole turns. The box says the landing frame. The object stays where it lands until a hand takes it again. Its values open in Adjust.

Throws that bounceright-click a held object > Throw or drop... > Bounces

The thrown object hits the walls, crates and floors of the place, bounces and rolls, and rests where physics leaves it. Bounce and Friction start from the object's own physical properties; Up to is the longest it can move. Lands and stops keeps the plain arc.

Hide from here / Show from hereright-click a held object

The object vanishes or comes back from this frame. The game follows it through Held objects in the game.

Trackright-click the limb > TrackShift+Left Shift+Right

Select whole track, Move 1 frame earlier, Move 1 frame later and Lock track work on the limb's row of keys, as under Key > Track (see Key). A locked track keeps its keys: they are not moved or deleted. Group has Track groups (the window), New group... and New group with all below..., for putting tracks in groups.

SliderPose > Slider > Open at the cursor, or right-click a key or limbT

A slider at the mouse that moves the pose between two poses while you watch: breakdowns, stronger or calmer poses, a bigger or smaller swing. With keys picked, it changes each picked key against its own neighbours. With none, it changes the picked limb, or the whole body, at the playhead.

  1. Press T. The slider opens by the mouse, in the 3D view or the Framez window, in the last mode. To pick a mode, choose it in Pose > Slider or press Shift with its letter.
  2. Move the mouse left or right. The pose follows. Hold Shift for fine moves; Ctrl snaps to 10 % and the thirds.
  3. Click or press Enter to key it. Right-click or press Esc to put the pose back.

Holding T while you move keys the pose when you let go. Tab or a chip changes the mode. = types an amount.

  • Breakdown (Shift+B): between the key before (-100 %) and the key after (+100 %), and past either to 150 %.
  • Blend to neighbour (Shift+N): from the pose as it is toward the key before or after.
  • Push / relax (Shift+H): relaxed onto what the keys either side give, or pushed away.
  • Blend to frame (Shift+J): click a frame on the ruler, then blend toward its pose, or away.
  • Scale amplitude (Shift+M): the picked keys' motion flat (-100 %) to twice (+100 %) about a pivot.
  • Blend to rest (Shift+U): toward rest, or toward the Poses window's pose named Default.
  • Time offset (Shift+O): the motion earlier or later through keys that stay on their frames. Loop wraps it in a looping clip.
  • Blend to ease: toward an eased line between the keys either side: slow start (-100 %) or slow end (+100 %).
  • Blend offset: the run of picked keys moves as one toward the key before (-100 %) or after (+100 %).
  • Shear: the run of picked keys leans: its start back toward the key before, or its end forward toward the key after.

Blend to ease, Blend offset and Shear have no key of their own: choose them in Pose > Slider or in Find a command (Ctrl+K).

The options row, also keys while the slider is open:

  • Local or World (W): for Breakdown, Blend to neighbour and Blend to frame.
  • Turn and Move (1, 2) and the axes X, Y and Z: which channels change.
  • Overshoot (O): lets the slider pass the ends.
  • Pivot (P): Scale amplitude's pivot: Average, Neighbour before, Neighbour after, Playhead or Rest.
  • Loop (L): Time offset's loop.
  • Falloff (F): spreads the change over a run of picked keys: Even, Middle (most in the middle), Start or End.

Each mode remembers its own options. Pose > Slider > Presets keys the mode the Inspector's slider shows at a fixed amount, at once. For Breakdown the presets are Before, 1/3, Half, 2/3 and After; each mode has its own.

breakdowns with snap, calmer or stronger poses, a bigger or smaller swing, easing back to a guard pose. R6, R15 and any Motor6D rig.

Mirror posePose > Mirror > Mirror pose, Mirror the picked keys

Swaps left and right for the whole body at the playhead, or copies one side onto the other. Mirror pose runs at the playhead with your last values. Mirror the picked keys runs over the frames of the picked keys. Its values open in Adjust, and the result is an item in the Made window.

  • Which way: Swap sides, Left onto right or Right onto left. It shows when Across is Side to side.
  • Across: Side to side, Front to back or Up and down.
  • Parts: Torso, Head, Arms and Legs. Legs left out stay planted when Feet planted is chosen in the Posing page.
  • Frames: the frames it covers.

Edit > Paste flipped pastes copied keys or a pose onto the other side, mirrored.

Mirror the whole clip / Mirror into a new clip...Pose > Mirror

Puts every key of every limb onto its twin, left and right swapped, the root's travel too. Easing, curves and holds go with the keys. Held objects change hands, events and face names swap Left and Right (Footstep_L becomes Footstep_R), pins go to the twin limb, and animation layers are mirrored too. One undo step. Mirror into a new clip... saves the mirror under a new name (SLASH_R gives SLASH_L) and opens it.

left and right versions of attacks, dodges and strafes from one clip.

Reset posePose

Puts every limb back at its rest pose and keys it at the playhead.

ConstraintsPose > Constraints, or right-click a limb > Constraints

Makes a limb aim at, go to, follow or copy something, over the frames you choose. The constraint redoes itself when what it follows changes, and keys you set by hand on the limb stay on top. Each one is an item in the Made window named Constraint.

  1. Pick the limb.
  2. Choose Pose > Constraints and one of the rows below. Its values open in Adjust.
  3. Choose the targets and their weights, and the frames the constraint covers.

The rows are starting points that fill in the values, and Kind in Adjust can change any of them:

  • Look at and Point at: Aim at the camera or a part picked in the Explorer.
  • Hand on it: Point with IK: a hand reaches the target and the arm bends.
  • Stay put: Follow where the limb is at the start.
  • Rope: Keep a distance, no closer than a minimum and no further than a maximum.
  • Above the floor: Stay above a height, with the floor as height 0.
  • Constraint: opens with the defaults.

The kinds: Aim points an axis of the limb at the target; Point sends its place to the target's; Orient matches its angle; Follow takes place and angle, as if parented; Copy takes another joint's own turn, with axes, flips and add or replace; Keep a distance; Stay above a height or the top of a part.

In Adjust, Kind and Limb pick what is constrained, and Add a target adds a limb, the selected object, the camera or a point. Targets are limbs of the rig, objects in the place, fixed points, the camera or where the limb starts. A constraint can have several, each with a weight. Influence is a curve over the frames: fade it in, hold it, let it go. An R15 hand or foot reaches its target with IK. Turn error and Move error set how closely the keys it writes follow every frame.

a head that follows the camera, a hand on a moving prop, a foot that stays above the floor, a limb that stays where it starts.

Draw a path / Draw a path on the groundPose > Constraints, or right-click a limb > Constraints

Draw one stroke in the 3D view and a limb, or the whole rig, follows it. The stroke is kept, so all of it can be changed later.

  1. Pick a limb. Pick none, or the root, to move the whole rig.
  2. Choose Draw a path to draw facing the camera through the limb, or Draw a path on the ground to draw on the ground under the rig.
  3. Drag one stroke in the view and let go. A constraint is made from the playhead to the end of the clip.

The whole rig follows the path with Face along the path on. An R15 hand or foot reaches along it (Point with IK). Any other limb points at it (Aim).

  • Path timing: Even, Ease in, Ease out or Ease in and out.
  • Your timing: a curve of your own.
  • Face along the path: turns the limb to face the way it travels.
  • Smoothing: 0 to 100.
  • Frames, Influence and Follow changes, as for any constraint.

A Pull, Blast, Lift or Black hole force in the Physics window can draw its own path.

DriverPose > Constraints > Driver

One value moves another: a joint's turn or place, a part's height or speed, a distance or the frame drives any channel of any limbs. The driver redoes itself when its input changes.

  • Input (From): A joint's turn, A joint's place, A part's height, A part's speed, Distance between two parts or The frame. Input from and Input to set its range; Hold at the ends stops it at both.
  • Map: a curve you draw from the input to the output, or a formula. In a formula, x is the input from 0 to 1, v its own value, t the time, f the frame and pi the constant 3.14159. Use + - * / ^ and the functions sin, cos, tan, abs, sqrt, min, max, clamp and floor.
  • Output: the channel (Rotation X, Y, Z, Position X, Y, Z) and the values it runs from and to. Mix: Add to its keys or Replace the channel.
  • Turn error and Move error: how closely the keys it writes follow every frame. Smaller keeps more keys.
  • Starting points: Turn follows turn, Lean with speed, Wheel turns with travel and Crouch with height.

a wheel that turns as the body travels, a lean that grows with speed, a crouch that follows height.

Pose controllerPose > Constraints > Pose controller

A slider or pad that blends limbs between poses you set. One drag moves many limbs at once, and the value is keyed over the clip.

  1. Choose Pose > Constraints > Pose controller. Name it and choose its Kind: Slider (Low and High) or Pad (two ways), with four ends: Left, Right, Down and Up.
  2. Add the limbs it moves.
  3. Pose the limbs on screen. They do not need keys. Then use the Set buttons, which take an end from the pose on screen: Set Low for one end, Set High for the other (Set Left, Set Right, Set Down and Set Up on a pad).
  4. Drag the controller in the Inspector's Controllers section. It keys at the playhead. You can also draw its curve over the clip in Adjust. Turn error and Move error set how closely the keys it writes follow every frame.

a hand that opens and closes with one slider, a head that looks left, right, up and down from a pad.

Physics window...Pose > Physics > Physics window..., or View > Windows > Physics

Runs one physics simulation over the clip and writes it as keys: a hit, a fall, wind, floating. You build it from bodies, forces and colliders. The window has a tree on the left (Body, Forces, Colliders, Settings) and a lane for each row over the whole clip on the right. Forces are diamonds or spans you drag in time.

  1. Choose Pose > Physics > Physics window.... Choose a starting point in Setups: Hit reaction, Ragdoll fall, Knocked back, Float, Blown by wind, or the one named Legs stay, body takes it. Setups keep their frames relative to the start. Your own setups can be saved too.
  2. In the tree, pick a Body part and set its values. Add forces with + on the Forces row (see Forces).
  3. Click Simulate. The result plays on the purple ghost. Tick Live to redo it as you change values.
  4. Click Write keys. The result becomes keys and a Physics item in the Made window.
  • Body: Head, Spine, Arms and Legs, and each opens to its parts. A part is Simulated, pulled toward its keys as strongly as its strength curve says (0 is limp), or Plays its keys, which holds it on its keys and pushes what it touches. Mass, friction, bounce and the joint's swing and twist are set per part.
  • Hold its place: how hard the whole body is pulled to where its keys put it in the world. 0 falls freely.
  • Colliders: Hits is The whole place or Picked objects only, and And the floor under the rig adds the floor.
  • Settings: Start at, Up to (seconds), Stop when it comes to rest, Keep the motion at the start, Gravity (Strength and Way: Down, Up, Forward, Back, Left or Right) and Blend back into the clip (From frame, Over).
  • Show: chooses what the 3D view draws: bodies (blue boxes), joint limits, forces (arrows) and colliders.

Ctrl or Shift+click picks a second body; right-click then offers Collide with each other. The picked row's values are on the Inspector's Physics page.

Colliders do not move with the animation of other rigs.

ForcesPhysics window > + on the Forces row

Pushes, winds, pulls and blasts that act on the simulated body. Each force is a row in the tree and a lane you drag in time.

  • Push: a hit. Pick the Part, then click Aim on the body and click where it is hit (it is pushed away from the camera), or set Around (0 from the front, 90 from the left, 180 from behind, 270 from the right), Up and Speed (studs/s). It can land At frame or last Over frames.
  • Wind: a way, a strength and gusts, over frames.
  • Pull: toward a point, over frames.
  • Blast: out from a point, at a frame or Over frames. Launch is an upward share and Spin turns it round the up axis.
  • Drag: thick air or water, over frames.
  • Lift: up, or any way, over frames.
  • Black hole: a pull that swirls round its centre. Spin is in turns a second.

Every force but Drag has a Radius (0 reaches everywhere) and a Falloff to the edge curve. A Pull, Blast, Lift or Black hole can ride a path: click Draw its path and draw on the ground, and its centre follows it from its first frame to its last. Clear its path removes it.

Turn about a pointPose > Physics

Spins, flips or rolls the whole body about a point. Choose the point: Left foot, Right foot, Weapon tip, Floor, a heel or toe, either hand, Its far end, a spot Alt+clicked in the view, or a placed point. Choose Spin, Flip or Roll, drag the Angle and the body turns as you drag, then click Key it.

a spin on one foot, a cartwheel, a pivot without the planted foot sliding.

Jump arcPose > Physics

The torso flies a true arc between take-off and landing at the height you set. The take-off and landing are the picked root keys, or the root keys either side of the playhead.

  • Roblox gravity: the top is as high as gravity allows over that time. The box says the time in the air and the height.
  • Height: the height of the top in studs, when Roblox gravity is off.
  • Starting points: Hop, Jump, High jump and Roblox gravity.

any jump.

Follow-throughPose > Physics

Follow-through is the motion that carries on after what moves a part has stopped: an arm after a swing, hair, a cape, a tail. This tool makes the head and arms (or the picked limb, or a whole chain of joints) trail behind what moves them, swing past and settle.

  1. Pick a limb, or the first joint of a tail, hair or cape.
  2. Choose Pose > Physics > Follow-through. Its values open in Adjust.
  3. Choose Way: Spring or Dynamics. Tick Whole chain to move every joint below the picked one.
  • Spring: a damped spring on each joint's turn. Lag (frames), Bounce (how far it swings past and back), Strength (how much is mixed into the motion), Spring give and Steps.
  • Whole chain: with it on, More lag at tip makes the end trail longer and Hang makes the chain droop toward the floor. They show when the picked joint has joints below it.
  • Dynamics: each joint's far end swings toward where the keys put it, hangs with gravity, blows in the wind and in the Physics window's forces, and stays out of the body, the floor and the picked objects. Stiffness, Stiffer at root, Drag, Gravity, Wind, Wind from, Gusts, Gust speed, Physics window's forces, Collide, Radius, Floor, Angle limit (0 is no limit), Steps, Warm-up (frames run before the clip starts, for a settled start) and Loop.
  • Loop: three choices. As the clip follows the clip's own loop setting. The middle choice runs the motion round twice and eases the end into the start, for a loop with no jump. Off never loops.
  • Starting points: Soft and Floppy (Spring), Hair or cape and Tail (Dynamics).

after a swing or a stop, a ponytail, a cape that blows, a tail that drags.

OverlapPose > Physics

Overlap makes parts start moving a few frames after what carries them. The head and arms (or the picked limb) take part of their parent's turn from a few frames before. Delay is the number of frames; Amount is how much of the parent's turn the limb gives back. Limbs picks which parts. Starting points: Loose, Tight and Heavy.

a spin where the head should lag, or a quick turn of the torso.

Plant a footPose > Physics

A foot stays exactly where it is over the chosen frames. The knee bends to keep it there. Choose the Foot and the frames. Starting points: Exact and Few keys.

every foot that should be on the ground and is not.

Fix all sliding feetPose > Physics, or the Check window

Finds every stretch a foot spends on the floor while it slides and plants it, as Plant a foot does, all at once. It says how many it fixed and how far the worst foot still moves. One Ctrl+Z takes it all back.

a clip from video, mocap or Blender, or after moving the hips.

Ragdoll...Pose > Physics, or right-click a limb > Ragdoll...

Pushes the body at a frame and lets Roblox physics take it: falls, deaths, knockdowns, hit reactions. The result is baked to keys.

  1. Go to the frame where the hit lands.
  2. Right-click the limb that is hit and choose Ragdoll... (or choose Pose > Physics > Ragdoll...).
  3. Under Start, set Start at. Tick Keep the motion at this frame to give the body the speed it had.
  4. Under Push, the first button names the part that is hit; click it to pick another part. Click Aim, then press on the body where the hit lands and drag the way it goes; a longer drag pushes harder. Or click Front, Back, Left, Right or Up and set Around, Up and Speed (studs/s).
  5. Under Body, click Limp, or Powered to keep part of the pose: a strength for Head, Spine, Arms and Legs, and Keep standing.
  6. Under Length, set Up to (seconds) and tick Stop when it comes to rest.
  7. Click Simulate. Scrub the timeline: the purple ghost plays the result.
  8. To return to the animation, tick Blend back under Back to the animation, and set At (the frame) and Over (the number of frames).
  9. Under Bake into, choose The keys or A new layer (an Add layer named Ragdoll; mute it to see the clip without the fall). Click Bake.

The rig does not move while it simulates: a copy falls and collides with the place's parts. Joints have limits; R15 elbows and knees bend one way. Locked limbs keep their keyed pose. Held objects stay in the hand. R6, R15 and other Motor6D rigs (generic limits).

Spin check / Fix the spinPose > Physics

Spin check: in each jump, teal ghosts show the turn and the height physics gives. A body turns faster tucked and slower stretched, and its centre of mass flies gravity's arc. Take-off and landing stay as keyed; no ghost shows where the keys already match.

Fix the spin: keys the torso through the air to match the ghosts.

  • Fix: Spin, Height or Both.
  • Every jump: fixes each jump found inside the frames. Floor within sets how near the floor counts as on it, and Least air how many frames apart take-off and landing must be.
  • Starting points: Spin, Height and Every jump.
Make a cyclePose > Generate

A walk, run, strafe or backpedal from a few numbers, already looping. R6 and R15.

a starting point to shape, instead of keying a cycle from nothing.

Cycle from my posesPose > Generate

A looping walk or run built from two poses you made: the contact pose (a foot reaching the ground) and the passing pose (the legs crossing). It plays contact, passing, the mirrored contact, the mirrored passing, then contact again.

  1. Pose and key the contact pose (the legs apart, one foot down) on one frame, and the passing pose (the legs passing under the body) on another.
  2. Choose Pose > Generate > Cycle from my poses. Its values open in Adjust.
  3. Set Contact pose and Passing pose to those two frames, then the rest.
  • Length: the cycle's length in frames. The clip becomes this long and loops.
  • Bounce: how far the root drops at the contacts and rises at the passings (studs).
  • Lean: forward lean of the root (degrees); minus leans back.
  • Travel: how far the root moves forward in one cycle, in studs. 0 stays in place.
  • Arm swing and Leg stride (%): scale how far each arm and leg moves from its average pose.
  • Easing: Linear or Cubic auto between the poses.
  • Starting points: In place and Moving forward.

a walk or run in your own character's style.

Add breathingPose > Generate

A slow rise and settle of the chest, shoulders and head on top of what is keyed.

Pose from videoPose > Generatehelper

Keys the torso, head, arms and legs from the person in the linked video, every few frames. Works from any camera angle.

Zoom the Reference window onto the person first. The result is a rough pass to clean up, not a finish.

Anticipation and overshootPose > Generate

Adds a wind-up before a move and an overshoot after it: the limb goes past its end pose and comes back. Pick the keys at the start and end of a move, or put the playhead inside it. Its values open in Adjust and the pose shows live on the rig.

  • Wind-up: the start pose is pulled back against the move by this share (%).
  • Wind-up frames: how long the start pose is held before the wind-up.
  • Overshoot: how far past the end pose it goes (%), and Overshoot frames: how long it takes to get there.
  • Settle: how many frames it takes to come back to the end pose.
  • Skip turns under and Skip moves under: limbs that move less than this over the move are left alone.
  • Starting points: Snappy, Heavy, Cartoon and Wind-up only.

a strike that reads stiff.

WavePose > Generate

A swing that travels down a chain of joints: a tail, a tentacle, hair, a cape. Each joint from the picked one down gets a Wiggle modifier in the Curves window. You can change it, switch it off or bake it into keys.

  1. Pick the first joint of the chain.
  2. Choose Pose > Generate > Wave. Its values open in Adjust.
  3. Set the look, or choose Swim, Sway, Tentacle or one of My looks.
  • Size: how big the swing is.
  • Joints per wave and Speed. On a looping clip the speed is Waves per loop.
  • Side to side, Up and down or Twist: which way the joints swing.
  • Bigger at tip, Even or Bigger at root: how the swing changes along the chain.
  • Root to tip or Tip to root: which way the wave travels.
  • Fade in and Die out: the swing starts gently and dies away.

Choose Wave again on a chain that has one to edit it. Size 0 removes it. A joint with no keys gets one at frame 0 where it stands. Saving bakes the wave; Apply in the Curves window bakes it into keys.

a tail wag, a swimming fish, a tentacle, hair in a breeze.

Motion signalPose > Generate

A repeating or random motion on any channel of any limbs: a wave, noise, steps, a curve you draw, or another limb's motion. It is written as keys over the frames you choose.

  • Source: Wave, Noise, Steps (random holds every few frames), Your curve or Another limb (its motion, Later by some frames).
  • Shape: Size (degrees on turns, studs on moves), Speed, Phase, Detail for noise, A step every, and Dies out. An envelope you draw over the frames fades it in, swells it or dies it out.
  • Spread: The same on every limb, Down the chain (each next limb later and bigger or smaller) or Random per limb.
  • Target: any of Rotation X, Y, Z and Position X, Y, Z on any limbs. Mix: Add to the motion, Scale the motion or replace it.
  • Starting points: Breathing, Swim, Sway, Tail wag, Fear shake and Hover.

breathing, a swimming tail, fear shaking, hovering.

Pose libraryView > Windows > Poses

Saves poses and keys them back: the whole body, a limb, the upper body or the legs. Click a picture to key it at the Blend amount (the Blend field runs from -100 to 200). Right-click for mirrored, rename, delete, Copy to My poses or Copy to this place.

Press a picture and drag sideways to blend. The press is 0 %, a cell's width is 100 %, up to 200 % to the right and down to -100 % to the left. Shift drags fine, Ctrl blends mirrored. Release keys the pose; Esc or the right button cancels.

The window has two sections: My poses (every place on this PC) and This place (this place only). Save pose > Save to picks the section.

A click or drag makes an item in the Made window at the playhead. Change its Pose, Blend, Mirrored, Only on (the limbs it keys) and its frames later. Over several frames the pose holds across them. Right-click a pose for Apply to the picked limb and Update from the rig.
