Get the helper

Edit

Find commands, undo, copy and paste poses, pick and delete keys, and open the settings and keyboard shortcuts.

Find a command...EditCtrl+K

Searches every menu command. Type what you want to do, and every command that matches is listed with the menu it lives in. Enter runs the first one. The list holds the commands of every workspace, this workspace's first. Running a command from another workspace switches to that workspace.

  1. Press Ctrl+K.
  2. Type a word of the command, such as mirror.
  3. Press Enter to run the first match, or click another.

finding a command without knowing its menu.

Right-click a result to give that command your own key (Change shortcut...).

Undo / RedoEditCtrl+Z Ctrl+Y

Takes back the last change, or puts it back. One run of a tool, or one whole drag of a value, is one undo step, however many keys it made. Adjust shows what Ctrl+Z brought back.

While an animation is open, Ctrl+Z belongs to Framez alone. Studio's own undo is off until the animation closes, and opening an animation clears Studio's undo list.

Copy pose / Paste pose / Paste flippedEdit, or the toolbar

Copies the picked keys with their spacing, or the whole pose when no keys are picked, and pastes them at the playhead. The rows read Copy 3 keys when 3 keys are picked, and Paste 3 keys when 3 keys are copied. Keys copied on every keyed row bring the events inside their stretch.

  • Copy pose: copies the picked keys, or the whole pose.
  • Paste pose: pastes at the playhead.
  • Paste flipped: pastes the copied keys or pose onto the other side, mirrored. Greyed until something is copied.

repeating a pose later in the clip, copying a left-side pose onto the right.

Copy and Paste are not on Ctrl+C / Ctrl+V. The rows are in the Animate workspace.

Paste onto this limbright-click a limb

Keys copied from one limb go onto the picked one. Left arm to right arm, for example.

World placeEdit > World place

Copies where a limb is in the world, then pastes it at another frame even though the torso moved. Every picked limb is copied and pasted.

  • Copy world place: copies where the picked limb is in the world.
  • Paste world place: puts the limb back at that place at the playhead. Greyed until a place is copied.
  • Paste onto selected keys: puts the limb at that place on every picked key. Greyed until a place is copied and keys are picked.

a hand that must stay on a ledge or a sword hilt while the body turns.

The rows are in the Animate workspace.

SelectEdit > Select, or right-click the timeline

Picks keys by rule instead of one by one. The same rows are in the timeline's right-click menu, where "this frame" means the frame you clicked.

  • Select all A: picks every key.
  • Select none Esc: lets go of every key.
  • Keys at this frame: every limb's key on the frame.
  • Keys before this frame: every key before the frame.
  • Keys after this frame: every key after the frame.
  • Keys between bookmarks: the keys between the bookmarks either side of the playhead.
  • Grow the selection: adds the key before and after each run of picked keys.
  • Shrink the selection: takes off each run's first and last key.

moving everything after a frame, deleting a stretch between two bookmarks, trimming a picked run.

Locked limbs are never selected. Deletes and drags leave their keys alone.

Delete keysEditDel

Deletes the picked keys. Greyed until keys are picked. Del also deletes the picked Made item.

A locked track keeps its keys on Delete.

Clip settingsEdit > Preferences

Opens the Inspector's Clip page: the clip's length, frame rate, priority, loop, the weapon and the published asset id, in one place. Greyed until an animation is open.

making a clip longer, setting a loop, setting the priority Roblox plays it at, reading the id a game script plays.

Settings...Edit > Preferences

Opens the Preferences window: every setting that is set once and kept. Changes apply at once. Each setting has its own reset, and number boxes take sums such as 2*pi or 90/4.

  1. Choose Edit > Preferences > Settings....
  2. Pick a category on the left. All lists every setting in category order.
  3. To find a setting, type in the search box. It looks through the setting names and what they do.
  • Handles and posing: Handle style (Screen size or Rig size); Size of the rings and arrows (Small, Normal or Large); Fine and snap (the speed of a Shift drag, and the steps of Ctrl's turn in degrees and Ctrl's move in studs); Auto key (On: letting go of a handle keys it. Off: the pose stays until you key it).
  • Timeline: Wheel zoom (how much one wheel notch zooms); Frame step (how many frames Alt+Left and Alt+Right move); Key jump (how many keys Previous key and Next key move).
  • New clips and keys: How a new key plays into the next (Linear keeps the keyed timing, Smooth eases through each key, As the key before copies its easing); New animations' Frame rate (1 to 240) and Length (0.1 to 600 s).
  • Navigation: 3D view (Studio or Blender); Orbit speed; Pan speed (%); Zoom toward the cursor (the wheel closes in on the point under the mouse).
  • Overlays: Motion paths' Length (Whole clip or Around the playhead), Frames each side (1 to 600) and Frame numbers (Off, Every key or Every 5 frames); onion skin Before colour and After colour (a hex code with a swatch) and Ghost strength (10 to 200 %); Bone style (Off, Octahedral or Stick) and X-ray.
  • Curves and drawing: Auto handles (Standard or Even acceleration); Pen smoothing (0 to 8, how many times a drawn stroke is smoothed when you let go; 0 keeps it as drawn).
  • Capture: the Green screen colour (Green, Blue or Your own, which is set in View > Camera > Green screen settings).
  • Saving: Recovery copy (Every 30 s, Every minute, Every 2 minutes or Off) and Kept (3, 5 or 10) for unsaved work; Versions kept (5, 10, 25 or 50) for History; Copies on disk, the folder where the Framez Helper copies each save (Documents\Framez backups until you set another).
  • Workspaces: Menus and tools. Its own: each workspace shows its own menus and tools. All: every menu, tool, track and Inspector section in every workspace.
  • Keyboard: opens Keyboard shortcuts....

With Navigation set to Blender, the 3D view orbits, pans and zooms with the middle mouse button and the number pad: see Blender navigation. The same switch is View > Camera > Blender navigation.

Keyboard shortcuts...Edit > Preferences

Opens a window that lists every command by menu, with its key, and lets you give any command your own key. Every row Find a command knows can take a key, plus the built-in keys such as Play, the arrows and the number pad. Menus and Find a command show your keys.

  1. Choose Edit > Preferences > Keyboard shortcuts....
  2. Find the command: type in the search box, or click By key and press keys to see what they do.
  3. Click the key shown on its row (a - when it has none).
  4. Press the new keys. Esc cancels.
  • Groups: Timeline, one group for each menu from File to Help, Curves, and the fixed keys that work while a tool is busy (the slider open, a camera path being drawn, a path key being dragged, the animation list).
  • A key that is taken: the row says so, for example "Shift+W is on Breakdown." with Replace and Cancel.
  • Studio's own keys: Studio's key runs as well, and the row says so ("Studio's Duplicate also runs.").
  • Several keys: a command can have more than one key. Right-click a row for Change, Add another key, Remove and Reset.
  • A changed row: shows a dot and its own Reset.
  • Start from: Framez, Blender, Moon Animator 2, or a set saved in this place. Starting over with changes asks "Replace them" or "Keep them on top".
  • The Blender set: I keys the limb, X or Delete deletes keys, Left and Right step a frame, Up and Down hop to the previous and next key, Shift+Left and Shift+Right go to the start and end, Esc or Alt+A picks none, Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes, F3 finds a command, Num . frames the limb, R turns, G moves, Shift+H shows only the picked limb.
  • The Moon Animator 2 set: = or Num + keys the limb, 4 and 6 step a frame, J and K hop to the previous and next key, B turns onion skin on and off, Num * adds a bookmark, Ctrl+R mirrors the whole clip.
  • Save mine to this place...: keeps your keys in the place under a name, and everyone working in it can start from them.
  • Reset all: asks "Undo your N changes?" and returns every key to the set you started from.

putting Play on a key you reach, moving a command to a key that suits you.

Esc and the Windows and Menu keys cannot be given to a command. Keys while a tool is busy, typing, and the animation list's Enter, F2 and Delete are never taken. The Curves group works only while you type in the Curves window: one key can do one thing there and another in the view.

Change shortcut...right-click any menu row or Find a command result

Gives that one command a key of your own, without opening the shortcuts window.

  1. Right-click the command in any menu, or in the results of Find a command.
  2. Choose Change shortcut....
  3. Press the keys in the "Press keys for NAME" box.
  • Add another key...: gives the command a second key.
  • Remove shortcut: takes its key away.
  • Reset shortcut: returns it to the key it started with.
  • Keyboard shortcuts...: opens the window on this command.

A key already on another command is named, with Replace and Cancel, as in the shortcuts window.
