Timeline and toolbar

The toolbar runs along the top of the Framez window. Under it the timeline has a row for every limb, and extra rows for events, layers, faces and more.

Play and steptoolbarSpace

Moves the playhead, the white line that marks the frame shown, and plays the clip.

  • Go to frame 0 (Home) and Go to the last key (End): jump to the start and to the last key of anything in the clip.
  • Previous key (←) and Next key (→): hop from key to key on any limb. Alt+← and Alt+→ step one frame.
  • Play / stop (Space): plays to the last key. Pressing play on the last frame starts again from frame 0. With View > Play only the picked keys on, it loops the stretch between the picked keys.
  • The ruler: click it to move the playhead; a key close to the click wins. Drag along it to reach any frame.

Edit > Preferences > Settings... > Timeline has Frame step (how many frames Alt+← moves) and Key jump (how many keys ← moves).

Loop, Key, Rotate, Move and World axestoolbar

Buttons for the commands used most while posing.

  • Loop the animation (Tab): the same as Loops.
  • Key the selected limb here (K): the same as Key this limb.
  • Rotate the limb and Move the limb (R switches between them): Rotate shows rings to drag, Move shows arrows to drag.
  • World axes (Ctrl+L): moves and turns along the world's axes instead of the limb's own.
View switches and the more buttontoolbar

Buttons that show or hide helpers over the rig and the timeline.

  • IK legs: a dragged foot bends the knee.
  • Onion skin: nearby keys as ghosts. Right-click for the capture rows.
  • Motion paths: the paths of hands, feet and the weapon. Right-click to pick which.
  • Reference video: recordings beside the timeline.
  • Curves: opens the Curves window.
  • The eye (P): shows or hides the handles. Right-click for the overlays.
  • More: in a narrow window these switches fold into one More button. It lists IK legs, Onion skin, Preview, Motion paths, Reference video and Curves.
Frame countthe middle of the toolbar

Shows the playhead's frame, the clip's length in frames and the time in seconds. Click it to type a frame, a length or a playing speed.

  • Frame: type 24, 0:24 or 0.4s to go there.
  • Length: type 50, 0.8s or 0:50 to set how long the clip is. It can be 0.05 s to 600 s.
  • Speed: how fast Framez plays, from 0.05 to 4. It applies here only and is not saved. View > Playback speed has the usual choices.
Zoom and fitright end of the toolbar, View > Timeline zoom

Shows fewer frames across the timeline for finer work, or the whole clip at once.

  • Zoom out (minus) and Zoom in (plus): step the zoom. The slider between them zooms as you drag it; right is closer. It hides when the window is narrow.
  • Show the whole timeline (the fit button): shows frame 0 to the end of the clip.
Tracks and keystimeline

A row per limb, with a diamond for every key. White diamonds are keys, blue ones breakdowns (poses between two keys that shape the move), small grey ones generated keys (made by Framez from the motion); the picked key is red.

  • Click a key: picks it, picks its limb and goes to its frame. Shift+click adds keys to the pick or takes them off.
  • Drag a key: moves it. Picked keys move together and keep their distances. A move that would pass frame 0 or the end, or land on a key that stays, goes as far as it can and the status line says what stopped it.
  • Drag on empty space in the rows: draws a box and picks every key inside it. Keys of locked limbs are not picked.
  • Right-click a key: Easing, Copy, Paste, Delete, Select whole track, Stretch timing..., and the Slider, Key type and Range submenus.
  • Right-click a track name: the limb's own menu, the same as a right-click on it in the 3D view.
  • Right-click empty space: moves the playhead there, then offers Key all limbs here, Copy pose, Paste, Select, Stretch timing..., Add event here..., Impact frame here, Flash here and Add bookmark here...

moving a pose a few frames, picking every key of one move to retime it.

Lock a limbright-click the limb > Track > Lock track

A locked limb can't be picked or changed, and always plays its keys. Unlock track (the same row) frees it. See Track.

protecting finished legs while working on the arms.

Face rowunder Head

For a head whose face is a Decal: each face key shows the face itself, its name and how many frames it holds, with a grey bar to the next key (a thin line when it is back to Own face). Click the empty lane to set a face there. Drag a key to move it; double-click it to change it; right-click for Copy face, Paste face here and Delete. A head with separate Eyes and Mouth Decals gets a row each. In a scene each rig has its own face keys, saved in its own clip; the rigs not being posed wear theirs as the timeline moves.

Events laneunder the ruler

The clip's events show here as pins with their names, beside weapon switches, sliding feet and the play range. Drag a pin to another frame; right-click it to rename or delete; double-click renames. The + on the Events row adds one at the playhead.

Layer weight rowunder the rig, Animate

For the layer being keyed and every layer with weight keys: its weight from 0 (the row's foot) to 100 % (its head), a diamond per key. Drag a key to move it in time and weight (Shift: weight only). Right-click a key for its Weight, Smooth, Linear or Hold to the next key, and Delete; double-click it for its weight. Right-click the row to key the weight there or remove the keys.

Made row and strength laneunder the rig, Animate

Shows what tools made in this clip as bars. Drag a bar to move a result in time or give it other frames after it is made. The row is named Made, with the number of items.

  • Click the row's name: opens the Made window with the items' values.
  • Drag a bar: its middle moves the item, an end changes its frames. The item is redone as you drag.
  • Double-click a bar: changes that item. Right-click a bar for the item's menu.
  • Strength lane: the picked item gets a lane under the row with its strength over its frames, 100 % at the head. Click the lane to add a point, drag a point (Shift: strength only), right-click a point to remove it.

See Run a tool, then change what it made.

Hit windows and Foot contacts rowsunder the rig, Game data

The hit windows as bars (overlapping ones stacked) and the foot contacts as a strip per foot, L and R, with a bar while the foot is down. Right-click either row to add.

  • Hit window bar: drag the middle to move it, an end to trim it. Right-click for Go to its start, Kind, Edit hitbox in the view, Duplicate and Delete.
  • Foot contact bar: drag to move it, trim an end, or right-click to delete it.
Parent rowunder a limb or object that has parent switches

A row named "<limb> · parent" shows which part a limb, a whole rig, a held object, a prop or a camera follows, and when it changes. Each switch made with Parent to... is a block named for its parent and coloured like it. World (stays put) is grey; a parent that has gone is red.

  • Click a block: goes to that switch.
  • Right-click a block: its menu.

The events lane names the ride, for example "RightHand on Head".

Track groupsthe track list, View > Windows > Track groups

A rig with many joints lists its tracks in groups, one for each branch (Body, Tail, Thigh.L) and any group you make. Groups turn on by themselves for rigs with mesh bones or 25 joints and more.

  • Click a group's row: folds or opens it. A folded group still shows the picked joint.
  • Double-click a group's row: keys every joint in it. The row's lane shows where any of its joints has a key.
  • The eye on a group's row: hides the group's joints in the view.
  • Right-click a group's row: Key every joint here, Only this group, Show all, Fold all groups, Open all groups, Rename... and Ungroup (your own groups), Tracks as a tree.
  • Right-click a joint > Track > Group: Track groups (switches groups on or off for the rig), New group..., New group with all below..., Put in a group (up to 5 of your groups are listed), Leave group.

animating a creature with dozens of bones, keeping a tail's joints in one place.

Subtitles rowScene workspace, after the scene's items

One block per subtitle line, coloured by who says it. Drag a block to move the line, drag an end to trim it. See Subtitles.
