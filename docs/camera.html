Camera Preview

The Camera preview shows what a scene camera sees while the viewport stays on another angle, so you can pose a character from any side and watch the shot at the same time. It opens as a floating window that you can dock or drag to a second screen, and it belongs to the Scene workspace.

Camera Preview

Menu
View ‣ Windows ‣ Camera preview
Right-click
A camera's row ‣ Preview it
Workspace
Scene

The window shows the camera the game sees at the playhead, following the cuts, or one camera that you choose at its top. It has three parts. A header along the top holds the shot selector, the camera's name and frame, and three buttons. The picture fills the middle, and a rail of five buttons beside it sets what dragging in the picture does. Drag inside the picture to fly the camera, and the scene camera moves while the picture follows.

Choosing Preview it on a camera's row opens the window with that camera chosen. With no camera in the scene, the window says "No camera in this scene" and offers a button that adds a camera where the viewport is.

The following sections describe the header, the picture and its rail, and the more menu.

Notes

The preview draws parts, meshes, decals, surface appearances, characters with their clothes, the sky and the terrain. It does not draw shadows, post effects, the atmosphere, particles, beams or trails. Those show when you look through the camera, which also shows the lines of the Subtitles row and camera shake.

The preview works from a copy of the place, made from the parts within 1,500 studs of the camera, up to 8,000 of them. Everything the scene moves, such as rigs and props, follows its original every frame. The rest of the copy is checked a few hundred parts at a time and updated when something is moved, resized or recoloured, and the whole copy is made again when the place has been still for two seconds after a change. The terrain is read again when the shot moves far from where it was copied. If the copy fails, Framez says so once and waits for a change in the place, or for Refresh, before trying again.

See Also

Look Through Camera shows the full shot in the viewport, and Framing sets the shape of the shot and the guides.

Header

The header holds the shot selector on the left. On the right it holds the name of the camera and the frame, then the eye, the Key button and the more button.

Shot Selector

Menu
Camera preview ‣ the button at the left of the header
Right-click
A camera's row ‣ Preview it
Workspace
Scene

The shot selector chooses which camera the window shows. Its button reads The shot while the window follows the cuts, and the camera's name while one camera is chosen. A chosen camera stays chosen until you choose another, and if that camera is removed from the scene the window goes back to The shot.

Beside the selector, the header names the camera that is showing and the frame, for example Camera frame 10. When you have flown the camera and not keyed it, the label adds "moved, K keys it".

Options

The shot (follows the cuts)
Shows whichever camera is live at the playhead, so the window changes camera at each cut.
A camera's name
Shows that camera alone, whatever the cuts say, so you can watch one angle while you pose.
Look through camera
Looks through the shown camera in the viewport, the same as pressing C. It shows a tick while you are looking. When the shown camera is not the live one at this frame, Framez says so, and the viewport shows the live camera.

Example

For example, pin the over-the-shoulder camera while you pose the character it follows, and keep the viewport on a side view where the handles are easy to reach.

Key

Menu
Camera preview ‣ the Key button in the header
Workspace
Scene

The Key button in the header keys the camera that is showing, with its place and field of view, at the playhead. It does the same as Key Camera, which also has the shortcut K. Use it after you have flown the camera with the modes of the rail.

The eye to its left is Clean view, and the three dots to its right open the more menu. Both are described below.

Picture and Modes

Picture

Workspace
Scene

The picture sits in a dark well with a margin on every side, at the shape of the shot, with bars where the window is a different shape. The shape follows the Aspect ratio in Framing, so a vertical short shows as a tall picture. The guides you have turned on and the scene's screen effects, meaning the letterbox bars, the vignette, the fade and the subtitle line, are drawn over the picture. The picture is lit by the place's sun direction and ambient light, scaled by Brightness. The water of the terrain shows as a tinted glass sheet.

Dragging in the picture flies the camera, using the mode that is filled on the rail. If the animation is playing, it stops when the drag begins. When you let go, the new place is keyed if Auto key is on. If it is off, a message says the camera moved and was not keyed, and K keys it.

See Also

Auto Key and Framing.

Modes

Workspace
Scene

The rail beside the picture holds five icon buttons, one for each way of flying the camera. Click one to make it the mode, and it shows filled. Orbit is the mode when the window first opens. Pointing at a button shows its name and what its drag does. Under the buttons, the rail shows the camera's field of view in degrees.

Options

Turn
Aims the camera where it stands. Dragging sideways turns it about the vertical axis, and dragging up or down tilts it.
Orbit
Circles the camera around what it looks at. The centre is the root of the rig that a Look at points the camera at, if there is one, or else the first thing in front of the camera within 300 studs, or a point 12 studs ahead when nothing is there.
Slide
Moves the camera sideways and up or down, so the scene moves with the pointer.
Dolly
Moves the camera nearer or further. Dragging up moves it forward, and dragging down moves it back.
Lens
Changes the field of view, from 1 to 120 degrees. Dragging down widens the view, and dragging up narrows it.

Example

For example, Orbit swings the camera round a character to find the best angle, then Dolly moves it in until the face fills the shot, and Key keeps the result.

More Menu

The three dots at the right of the header open the more menu, which holds the guides, the preview's brightness, Clean view and Refresh.

Guides

Menu
Camera preview ‣ more menu ‣ Guides
Workspace
Scene

Guides opens the same list as the Framing menu of the Scene menu: Aspect ratio, Show the frame, Thirds, Safe areas, More guides, Guide sets and Guide settings. The guides draw over the picture, and Aspect ratio reshapes it. They are the same guides that draw while you look through the camera, with the same settings, so a change made here shows in both places. Clean view hides the guides along with the controls.

See Also

Framing describes each row of the list.

Brightness

Menu
Camera preview ‣ more menu ‣ Brightness
Workspace
Scene

Brightness changes how brightly the preview is lit. It affects only the preview. What the game shows is lit by the place itself. The level is remembered by Studio.

Options

70%, 85%, 100%, 115%, 130%, 150%
The preview's light as a share of its normal level. 100% is the normal level and the default.

Clean View

Menu
Camera preview ‣ eye button in the header
Camera preview ‣ more menu ‣ Clean view
Workspace
Scene

Clean view hides the header, the rail and the guides, so the picture fills the whole window. Click the eye in the header, or choose Clean view in the more menu, to turn it on. To bring the controls back, move the mouse over the window and click the eye that appears in its top right corner.

Notes

The eye in the corner shows only while the mouse is over the window, so it does not sit on the picture. The setting is remembered by Studio, and the window opens the same way next time.

Example

For example, put the window on a second screen in Clean view as a monitor for the shot while you work in the main window.

Refresh

Menu
Camera preview ‣ more menu ‣ Refresh
Workspace
Scene

Refresh makes the preview's copy of the place, and of the terrain, again straight away. The preview already updates itself when parts move, resize or change colour, and it copies the place again when the place has been still for two seconds after a change. Use Refresh after large changes, such as inserting a model or reshaping the terrain, when you want the picture to catch up at once.
