Menus/File
Get the helper

File

Start, open and save animations, bring clips in from other tools, send them out, and set up the rigs to animate.

New animationFile

Starts a new, empty animation on the picked rig. The rig goes back to its rest pose, the name goes back to Untitled, and loop, events and layers start clear. The rig shows its own face again. The frame rate and length come from Edit > Preferences > Settings... (New clips and keys).

  1. Click the rig in the 3D view or in the list Framez shows.
  2. Choose File > New animation.

With unsaved changes it asks "Save changes to NAME first?" with Save (Save as... for an untitled animation), Do not save and Cancel. In a scene it asks before closing the scene. One Ctrl+Z takes it back.

Open...File

Lists this rig's saves. Pick one to open it. The list sorts by name, length, keys, loops, priority and when each was saved.

  • Double-click: opens the save.
  • F2: renames the picked save.
  • Right-click: duplicates the save, deletes it, or shows its History.

carrying on with an earlier animation, duplicating a save before a risky change.

Opening one of a scene's clips opens the whole scene. With unsaved work, Open... asks first, as New animation does.

SaveFileCtrl+S

Saves the animation into the place, under the rig's name in ServerStorage, with the name in the header. An animation still called Untitled opens Save as... first. In a scene it saves every rig's clip and the scene itself (see Saving a scene).

keeping a take before trying something else, saving a scene's clips together.

Then save the place itself. An unsaved place loses the save when Studio closes. An animation with no keys is not saved. A span with an easing Roblox cannot store is saved as a pose on every frame, and opening the save in Framez brings back the keys and curves you made. In Team Create, saving over a clip someone else saved since you opened it asks first (see Team).

Save as...File

Saves the animation under a name you type, next to this rig's other saves. The animation then carries the new name.

  1. Choose File > Save as....
  2. Type the Name.
  3. Press Enter.

keeping a version beside the original, giving a first draft its real name.

A name that is already saved asks "NAME is already saved. Replace it?" with Replace and Cancel.

Publish to Roblox...File

Saves the animation and opens Roblox's upload window for it. After you submit, Framez lists your animations on Roblox. The one you pick becomes this animation's asset id, kept with the animation for game scripts.

  1. Choose File > Publish to Roblox....
  2. In Roblox's upload window, set Creator to whoever owns the game, then submit.
  3. Back in Framez, click Get the ID and pick the animation in the list.
  • Get the ID: lists your animations on Roblox. The one you pick is this animation's id.
  • Later: closes the box. The id can be picked on the Inspector's Clip page (Edit > Preferences > Clip settings).

getting the asset id a game script plays, publishing a skill's animation.

An untitled animation opens Save as... first, and an animation with no keys is not published. Framez opens Roblox's upload window and does not upload on its own.

History...File, or right-click a save in Open

Every Save keeps a version of the animation: the last 10, or as many as Preferences says (5, 10, 25 or 50). The list shows each version's number (v1, v2...), when it was saved and how many keys it had; the one saved last is ticked. Pick another to restore it. The save it replaces stays in the list and can be restored the same way.

going back to how it was an hour ago, or to before a bad change.

The versions are kept in the place (ServerStorage.FramezHistory). An unsaved place loses them. While the Framez Helper runs, each save is also written to disk as a .json and an .rbxm. Drag the .rbxm into ServerStorage.RBX_ANIMSAVES and the rig's folder to get it back. The folder is set in Preferences (Saving).

Roblox animation...File > Import

Puts a Roblox animation on this rig as keys. R6 clips go onto R15 rigs and R15 clips onto R6, limb by limb.

  1. Choose File > Import > Roblox animation....
  2. Paste an asset id or its link, or pick an Animation, KeyframeSequence or CurveAnimation already in the place.
  3. Replace the clip, or add it at the playhead.

starting from a catalog animation, an emote, or a clip made for another rig.

Replacing the open clip asks first. One Ctrl+Z takes the import back.

Moon Animator 2 file...File > Import

Opens a Moon Animator 2 save from this place: the rig's keys with their easing styles, plus the other rigs, cameras and parts in the save as scene items with their keys.

  1. Choose File > Import > Moon Animator 2 file....
  2. Pick a save in the list. It holds every save in ServerStorage and ReplicatedStorage under MoonAnimator2Saves or MoonAnimatorSaves, newest first, nine at a time with More.
  • The rig: its keys go on the open rig. The rig's own CFrame track goes into the root joint.
  • Other rigs: join the scene with their keys.
  • Cameras: become scene cameras. Parts and models become props, with any keyed property. Emitters become effects.
  • Markers: become events. A marker with a width also gets an event named <name>End.
  • Not opened: camera attach and look-at keys, sound and humanoid actions, terrain colours and bones. The message lists what was left out.

moving a project from Moon Animator 2 to Framez without exporting each clip.

With no save in the place, the message reads "No Moon Animator 2 files in this place."

FBX or Mixamo clip...File > Importhelper

Brings an FBX or BVH clip (Mixamo, Unreal, a Roblox R15 or R6 export, mocap) onto this rig through Blender. Framez sends the rig to Blender, Blender puts the clip on it, and Get from Blender brings it back.

  1. Choose File > Import > FBX or Mixamo clip....
  2. In Blender's Framez tab, click Import a clip... and pick the FBX or BVH file.
  3. In Framez, choose File > Import > Get from Blender.

Mixamo clips, mocap, anything made in another 3D app.

In place is on by default: the hips keep their up and down but not their travel. Needs the Framez Helper and the Framez tab in Blender (see Helper and Blender setup).

Get from BlenderFile > Importhelper

Brings back what Blender's Send to Framez sent, as keys on the open animation. Where Blender marks its keys, those frames become keys with Blender's easing, and Framez adds a key wherever its own curve would differ from Blender's pose. A pose on every frame with no key list is thinned to the keys it needs.

  • Events, weapon switches and face keys: go to their markers' frames. One whose marker was deleted stays where it was.
  • Joints Blender does not know: are listed and left alone.
  • Face shape keys: shape keys in Blender named like the head's face controls (any case, spaces, dots or underscores, with or without a FACS prefix) become keys on the head's face controls, in one undo step. A channel Blender does not key is left alone. A head with no face controls says so.
  • Rig setups: bone constraints and drivers sent with Send the rig setup come in too (see Rig setups from Blender).

polishing a clip in Blender and bringing the result back, importing a clip made in Blender.

Rig setups from BlenderBlender: Framez tab > Send the rig setup, then File > Import > Get from Blenderhelper

Brings a Blender armature's constraints, limits and drivers onto the joints of the same names. Each constraint or driver becomes an item in the Made window on its joint, changeable and undoable like any other.

  1. In Blender, select the armature.
  2. In the 3D View, open the N panel, go to Framez and click Send the rig setup.
  3. In Framez, choose File > Import > Get from Blender.
  • Copy Rotation: becomes a Constraint of kind Copy.
  • Copy Location: becomes Point.
  • Damped Track, Track To, Locked Track: become Aim.
  • Child Of: becomes Follow.
  • Limit Distance: becomes Keep a distance.
  • Floor: becomes Stay above its object.
  • Limit Rotation: becomes the joint's limit (a hinge, or a cone and a twist).
  • IK: becomes an IK chain.
  • A bone driver on rotation or location: becomes a Driver. The expression becomes its formula, set to Replace and live.

keeping a Blender rig's behaviour when its animation moves to Roblox.

Influence carries over. Muted constraints are left out. Anything with no match is named in the status line. Needs the Framez Helper and Blender's Framez tab (see Helper and Blender setup).

A scene from the game...File > Import

Puts a scene that was put in the game back into this place's saves. The scene's record comes back, with every rig's clip that this place lost.

  1. Choose File > Import > A scene from the game....
  2. Pick the scene's name. The list holds the scenes in ReplicatedStorage.FramezScenes.
  3. Choose File > Open and pick the scene.

getting a scene back after this place lost its saves.

With no scene in the game, the row reads "No Framez scene in the game". Scenes get there with Put in the game....

Unsaved work and recoveryEdit > Preferences > Saving, the start screen, File > Import > Recover unsaved work...

While an animation is unsaved, Framez keeps a recovery copy a few seconds after the last change. Edit > Preferences > Saving sets how often it is made (Every 30 s, Every minute, Every 2 minutes or Off) and how many copies are kept (3, 5 or 10). In a scene, the posed rig's clip is copied.

The next time Framez opens, the start screen shows a card: Unsaved work, with the animation and rig, and the buttons Recover and Discard (Discard asks first). File > Import > Recover unsaved work... is shown while copies exist and lists them. Recover opens the copy on its rig, unsaved, with the asset id back. A Save, or closing the animation, removes its copy.

getting work back after Studio closes or crashes before a Save.

A recovery copy is not a save. Save the animation, then the place.

Emote...File > Export

Checks this clip against Roblox's rules for UGC emotes and saves it as an emote. Each rule is a row: red where Roblox would refuse the upload, grey for what Save leaves out, green where it passes (Show passed lists those).

  • The rules: a standard R15, at most 10 s, every body part at most 5 studs from the root part and no more than 3.1 under it, nothing faster than 30 studs a second, and one event.
  • Length: the fix is Stretch or Trim.
  • Too far, Too low, Too fast: the fix is Pull back. It scales the root's travel back towards the start, then lifts the root where the body sinks too low.
  • No one-click fix: the row has Go to, which jumps to the frame.
  • Fix all: runs every fix in one undo step.
  • Save as emote (bottom right): writes NAME_EMOTE, the CurveAnimation an emote upload needs, then shows Roblox's two upload steps as cards. Framez spots the Animation Roblox's upload window makes in Workspace and opens the Avatar Item upload on it.

anything sold as an emote, before paying the upload fee.

Joints a standard R15 hasn't got (a weapon), and events after the first, are left out of the emote and listed as notes. Framez opens Roblox's upload window and does not upload. Pick Emote and submit there. In the emote, a hold eases over one 30 fps frame.

Animation pack...File > Export

Builds a set of clips for the Marketplace or for this game. The window has a slot for each clip of a pack: Idle, Idle 2, Walk, Run, Jump, Fall, Climb, Swim and Swim idle, each a save of the open rig, with a weight for each idle. Packs are kept in the place (ServerStorage.FramezPacks). Packs switches between them, and a new name starts a new one.

  • Check: opens each clip in turn and lists what the Marketplace refuses: not an R15; a clip that does not loop (Jump must not); longer than 10 s; any part but LowerTorso moving; LowerTorso more than 1.5 studs from its start; and Roblox's reach, height and speed limits (as Emote... checks them). Each row has its fix, which saves the clip: Set its loop, Keep only turns, Pull LowerTorso in, Fit and pull back. The clip that was open opens again.
  • For the Marketplace: makes each clip a CurveAnimation named after the pack and its slot, in the pack's folder, and picks them in the Explorer. The window then lists what Save to Roblox asks for each clip: Animation type, Sub animation, Weight and Parent model name.
  • Use in this game: adds a LocalScript, StarterCharacterScripts.FramezMovement, which gives every character the pack's clips through its Animate script (R15 and R6 games). Each clip must be published first.

Save the open clip first: Check and the fixes open each clip.

Save as additive...File > Export

Saves this clip as NAME_ADD: every pose minus a reference pose (frame 0, the frame at the playhead, or rest). In a Roblox Animation Graph, the Add node plays it over another animation and moves each joint by the same amount.

breathing, a recoil, a flinch or a lean that should play over any animation.

Make the layer over the pose it belongs to (the stance), and pick that frame as the reference. Events and weapon switches are not copied into it.

Send to BlenderFile > Exporthelper

Sends the rig and the open clip to Blender: an armature with a bone per joint, boxes for the parts, the pose on every frame, and which frames are keys with how each eases. Events, weapon switches, face keys, hit windows and foot contacts arrive as timeline markers. In Blender, the Framez tab's Get from Framez builds it.

polishing with Blender's tools, or keeping a Blender copy.

Layers, clip blocks and curve modifiers stay in Framez. Send says so when they change what Framez shows. Needs the Framez Helper and the Framez tab in Blender.

Aim offset poses...File > Export

Saves nine aim poses from the pose at the playhead (left, right, up, down and the corners) and aims them live: drag Yaw and Pitch. Every value is under Aim offset poses... in Game.

a character whose upper body follows the camera or a target while the legs play their own animation.

The row is in the Animate workspace.

Live link with BlenderFile > Exporthelper

Keeps the open animation and Blender in step while you work: an edit in Framez shows in Blender, and an edit in Blender shows in Framez. Keys stay keys both ways, with their easing and key type. Where one program cannot draw the other's curve exactly, a Generated key is added.

  1. Open the animation.
  2. Choose File > Export > Live link with Blender. A tick shows while it is on.
  3. In Blender, open the Framez tab and click Live.
  4. Edit in either program.
  • Framez to Blender: Framez sends a moment after an edit stops, and keeps sending while a drag goes on.
  • Blender to Framez: only the joints that changed are written. The message reads "Blender: N joints changed." It is one undo step, and the playhead and view stay where they were.
  • Markers: events, weapon switches, face keys, hit windows and foot contacts travel as timeline markers and follow retimes.
  • Stays in Framez: layers, clip blocks and curve modifiers.
  • Off: choose the row again.

polishing curves in Blender's Graph Editor while watching the result in your game's scene.

Needs the Framez Helper and the Framez tab in Blender (see Helper and Blender setup).

Playblast...File > Exporthelper

Records the animation from Studio's 3D view to a video. The dialog opens with the last settings. Record starts it. Esc stops it and deletes what was written. When it finishes, the video opens, and a small menu offers Show in folder.

  • Camera: Studio's view, or the scene's camera.
  • Frames: the whole clip, the play range, or From and To.
  • Size: as the view, 1280 x 720 or 1920 x 1080.
  • Frame rate: the clip's, or 30.
  • Frame number, No number: draws the frame number in a corner, or not.
  • Onion skin, No onion skin: includes the onion skin, or not.
  • Reference beside it, Animation only: places the linked video next to the recording, or records the animation alone.
  • Format: MP4 video or PNG frames.
  • Folder: where the video or frames are written.
  • Open when done, Do not open: whether the video opens when it finishes.

showing a clip to someone, or keeping a take beside the video it copies.

Needs the Framez Helper running. The first time, Studio asks to allow screenshots. Keep Studio's 3D view on screen while it records. The row is in the Animate and Scene workspaces.

New R6 rig / New R15 rig / My avatar as R6 / My avatar as R15File > Rig

Puts a rig on the ground in front of the camera, facing it, and picks it: Roblox's own R6 or R15, or the Studio account's avatar as R6 or R15. R15s get Motor6Ds and open at once. Each gets a name no other rig uses. In an open scene, the new rig joins the scene.

starting on a fresh character, or animating the Studio account's avatar.

Rig a whole modelFile > Rig

Gives the model picked in the Explorer (a door, a chest, a creature) a joint on every piece. Pieces welded together stay together. The model also gets an AnimationController, which plays it in the game.

animating a door, a chest, a creature or a machine that is not a character.

Join selected parts... / Unjoin selected jointFile > Rig

Join adds a Motor6D between two parts, ready to animate. Unjoin turns a picked Motor6D into a weld: the part stays on and stops animating.

  1. With no animation open, pick two parts in the Explorer with Ctrl+click: first the one that holds, then the one that moves. An accessory counts as its Handle, a model as its main part.
  2. Choose File > Rig > Join selected parts....
  3. Pick where it turns: its own centre, the holder's, or halfway.

a sword into a hand, a cape on the torso, a tail or wing on a creature, a prop the character carries.

Do it with no animation open. Studio's Ctrl+Z undoes it, and does not work while an animation is open. Move the turning point later with Edit pivot.

Map parts...File > Rig

Opens the Rig map window: the body with a dot for each part. White dots are mapped, grey ones optional, red ones needed. Done saves the map on the rig's joints. Every clip and every place keep it.

  1. Choose File > Rig > Map parts....
  2. Click Auto map to fill the dots from the parts' names and the rig's shape.
  3. To fill a dot by hand, click it, then click the part in the 3D view, or choose the part from the list under the figure.
  4. Click Done.
  • Clear a dot: right-click it.
  • Legs bend: sets Forward or Back for the knees.
  • Extra chains: click Add chain, then click the chain's first part and its last.

creatures, mechs and characters whose parts are not named like Roblox's, so IK, mirror, generators and Import work on them. R6 and R15 rigs are mapped by their names.

Put back deleted rigFile > Rig

Puts back the rig that was deleted while its animation was open, where it stood, and opens it again. Studio's own Ctrl+Z cannot bring it back while an animation is open.

recovering a rig that was deleted by mistake while it was open.

The row is greyed (No rig has been deleted) until a rig is deleted. After a delete it reads Put back and the rig's name, and the status line says so. If Studio destroyed the rig, the status line says it cannot be put back.

Mesh bonesa rig with bones, File > Rig

Animates creatures, custom avatars, capes, tails, hair and wings made as one skinned mesh, through their bones. Click the model and open an animation: its bones show through the body.

  • Pick a bone: click a drawn bone, or the skin over it. The bone under the cursor turns white and the picked bone is orange.
  • Every tool works on bones: keys, curves, layers, the slider, paths, onion skin, mirror, IK chains through the rig map, Import, effects and held objects on a bone.
  • Mirror: finds a bone's twin by the sides in its name: .L and .R, _L and _R, Left and Right, L_ and R_.
  • Welded meshes: a skinned mesh welded to a part, such as a tail on a torso, rides that part and opens with the rig.
  • Bones overlay: a rig with bones opens with Bones on (Octahedral, with X-ray).
  • Track groups: a rig with many joints lists its tracks in groups, one for each branch (View > Windows > Track groups).
  • In the game: saves play with no script.
  • Left out: bones named like a body part, face bones of a dynamic head, tip bones ending in _end or .end, and bones in accessories.
  • Parts only: Edit pivot and squash and stretch work on parts only. For a bone, use Pivot > Turn about.

a dragon with a bone tail, a cape or hair on a skinned avatar, a creature with wings.

Close animationFile

Lets go of the rig. With unsaved work it asks "Save changes to NAME first?" with Save, Do not save and Cancel. In a scene it asks before closing the scene.
