Get the helper

Key

Save poses as keys, change how the moves between them play, change when they happen, and clean up the result. Every row here is in the Key menu of the Animate workspace.

Key this limb / Key all limbsKeyK

Saves a key on the playhead frame. Key this limb saves the picked limb exactly as it stands; Key all limbs saves every limb of the rig.

  1. Move the playhead to the frame.
  2. Click a limb in the view, or its name in the track list.
  3. Choose Key > Key this limb, or press K. Choose Key all limbs to key the whole rig.

holding a pose (key it again a few frames later), setting a first pose on every limb.

Key this limb is also the first row of a limb's right-click menu. Right-click empty space on the timeline for Key all limbs here. In a scene K keys the picked camera, prop or light.

EasingKey, or right-click a key7

Changes how the picked keys move into the next ones: where the limb speeds up and slows down on the way. An easing belongs to the key that starts the move. One pick can change one move or many at once.

  1. Pick one key or several: click, Shift+click, or drag a box on the timeline.
  2. Press 7, or choose Key > Easing. A menu opens at the cursor.
  3. Click one of the four buttons on top to say where the move slows.
  4. Click a style in one of the three columns. It applies at once as one undo step.
  • Slow start, Slow end, Slow start and end, Slow middle: where the move slows. Slow start begins slowly, Slow end arrives slowly, Slow start and end does both, Slow middle is fast at both ends and slow between. The last choice is kept.
  • Plain: Hold keeps the pose until the next key and then jumps to the next pose; Linear moves at a steady speed.
  • Gentle to strong: Sine, Quad, Cubic, Quart, Quint, Sextic, Expo and Circ, from the gentlest curve to the strongest.
  • Bouncy: Back goes past the next key and comes back (an overshoot), Bounce, and Elastic. Elastic then opens Adjust with its two amounts.
  • Extra turns: a whole number from -4 to 4. It adds that many full spins to every picked move; a minus number spins the other way. Use it for spins past half a turn.
  • My easings: the easings you saved sit under the styles as buttons. Click one to give it to the picked moves. Right-click one for Use for new keys (new keys get it) or Delete.
  • Save as mine: asks for a name and keeps the first picked move's easing: its style, where it slows and Elastic's amounts.

a hit that snaps in and settles slowly, a limb that comes to rest after a swing, a spin of several turns.

Point at a style to see it on the rig and the timeline before you click; moving away puts the keys back. The Inspector's key page has the same choice for one key: Easing and Extra turns.

Stretch timing...Key > Timing, or right-click a key

Makes the picked keys, or the whole clip, faster or slower: the keys move closer together or further apart. Stretching the whole clip moves the events, weapon switches and face keys with it.

  1. Pick two keys or more. With none picked, or one, the whole clip stretches.
  2. Choose Key > Timing > Stretch timing... The box says which keys it will move and their frames.
  3. Type a Scale: a factor (x2, 50%) or a length (0.55s, 33f).
  4. Click one of First key, Last key, Middle or Playhead. That frame stays where it is.
  5. Click Scale, or press Enter.

a skill that must last exactly as long as its cast time, a dodge that must last 0.4 s.

A stretch that would put a key before frame 0 changes nothing and says so: pick another pivot or scale less. A clip that grows gets a longer timeline.

Move picked keys...Key > TimingShift+← Shift+→

Moves the picked keys earlier or later by a number of frames you type.

  1. Pick the keys.
  2. Choose Key > Timing > Move picked keys...
  3. Type the number of frames in By: a minus number moves them earlier.
  4. Click Move.

sliding a whole pose 6 frames later, pushing a hit one frame earlier.

Shift+← and Shift+→ move the picked keys one frame. Nothing moves when the first picked key is on frame 0, the last one is on the clip's last frame, or another key is in the way, and the message says which. Keys on a locked limb never move.

Ripple editsKey > TimingShift

When on, retiming moves what comes after it. Stretching the box's right handle, or dragging keys, moves every key after them (on every limb), and the events, switches and face keys after them, by as much; the clip's length follows. Shift while dragging turns it on or off for that drag.

retiming one hit in a combo and keeping the rest in step.

Retime markersKey > Timing

Speeds up or slows down stretches of the clip by dragging markers, without typing a factor. A strip over the events lane holds the markers; the keys between two markers spread or squeeze, and events and switches go with them.

  1. Choose Key > Timing > Retime markers. The strip appears over the events lane.
  2. Click the strip to drop a marker.
  3. Drag a marker. The keys between it and its neighbours spread or squeeze on each side. Each stretch shows its speed (77 %, 250 %).
  4. Right-click a stretch for Ease in, Ease out or Ease in and out, which space its keys on a curve. Right-click a marker to delete it.
  • Ease retime stretch (Key > Timing > Ease retime stretch > Ease in, Ease out, Ease in and out): eases the stretch between markers that holds the playhead.

matching the reference's speed changes without moving keys one by one.

With keys picked, markers move only the picked limbs' keys. The markers are kept with the Framez data.

Add a frame here / Remove a frame hereKey > Timing

Every key after the playhead moves one frame later or earlier.

a hold that is a frame too short or too long.

Offset limbsKey > Timing

Staggers the picked keys: each limb's keys land a few frames after the limb above it. That is overlap: parts that start moving a few frames after what carries them.

  1. Pick keys on two limbs or more.
  2. Choose Key > Timing > Offset limbs. It runs at once and its values open in Adjust.
  3. Change Frames between, or pick another Order.
  • Frames between: how many frames later each limb's keys start than the one before it. A minus number goes the other way.
  • Order: The rig's order (root first), Reversed or As listed.
  • Setups: the Setups row in Adjust lists Overlap down the arm, Drag behind.

arms that follow the hips, a wave down the arm.

Snap keys to video beatsKey > Timinghelper

Moves each picked key onto the nearest still moment or sound hit in the linked video, up to 8 frames away. It never overwrites another key.

Stretch by draggingthe box round the picked keys

Picked keys on two frames or more get a box with a handle at each end; the label over it shows the length. Drag the right handle to stretch or squeeze them toward the left end, the left handle toward the right end. Hold Alt while dragging to stretch about the middle. Keys land on whole frames and never on each other: the drag stops at the shortest length that keeps every key. Right-click while dragging cancels. One undo step.

a swing that is too slow or too fast, without typing a factor.

ReverseKey > Range, or right-click a key > Range

The picked keys play backwards inside their stretch, every frame exact: an Ease In becomes an Ease Out, a hold holds the other pose. With nothing picked, the whole clip. Events, weapon switches and face keys inside reverse too.

a sheathe from a draw, a recovery from a wind-up.

Repeat / Fill to the playhead / Ping-pongKey > Range

Adds copies of the picked keys after them. Each copy starts on the last frame of the one before: a stretch that loops repeats without a seam. Events and switches inside are copied into every copy.

  1. Pick keys on two frames or more.
  2. Choose Key > Range > Repeat. It runs at once and its values open in Adjust.
  3. Set Times (1 to 50 more copies) and pick Insert or Over keys after.
  • Insert: the keys after the stretch move later to make room.
  • Over keys after: the copies replace whatever is there.
  • Fill to the playhead: copies up to the playhead, cut there with a key.
  • Ping-pong: the stretch, then the same stretch backwards.

channelled skills, idles, a shake or a loop inside a longer clip.

Delete range / Crop to rangeKey > Range

Delete range removes the keys inside a range and keeps the motion either side. Crop to range keeps only the frames from From frame to To frame and cuts the rest; the clip's length becomes the range's. Both add a key at each edge.

  1. Choose Key > Range > Delete range or Crop to range. It runs at once and its values open in Adjust.
  2. Set From frame and To frame. They start at the picked keys, the play range or the playhead.
  • Every limb or the picked limb's name (Delete range): which limbs lose their keys.
  • Start at frame 0 or Keep the frames (Crop to range): move the kept part to the start of the clip, or leave it on its frames.

cutting a clip down, or a take out of a longer recording.

Bake every N framesKey > Range

Replaces the keys with a key every N frames (1 to 8), read from the motion as it plays, over the picked keys' stretch or the whole clip. The new keys are Generated keys: small grey keys Framez made from the motion.

  1. Pick the keys whose stretch to bake, or none for the whole clip.
  2. Choose Key > Range > Bake every N frames. It runs at once and its values open in Adjust.
  3. Set Every (the number of frames) and pick Hold each pose or Smooth between.
  • Hold each pose: each pose holds until the next key. The clip steps in the game too.
  • Smooth between: the motion between the keys is kept.

a stylised, stop-motion look.

Reshape keysKey > Range

Exaggerates, calms, shifts or retimes the motion of many keys at once, with curves that change over time. Framez keys every frame of the range and then thins the result to the keys the motion needs.

  1. Choose Key > Range > Reshape keys. It runs at once and its values open in Adjust.
  2. Pick the Limbs and the Frames (at least 3 frames), and tick the Channels to change.
  3. Draw the curves under Over time.
  • Channels: Rotation X, Y, Z and Position X, Y, Z.
  • Amount over time: the middle line keeps the motion as it is, the top doubles it, the bottom flattens it to its average.
  • Shift over time: above the middle line adds to the channels, below takes away, up to Most turn (degrees) and Most move (studs; shown when a position channel is ticked).
  • Time warp: how far through the frames each frame plays. A steeper line plays faster, a flatter line slower.
  • Setups: the Setups row in Adjust lists Exaggerate, Calm down, Double, Flatten.

a swing that needs more punch, a held pose that needs to move less, speeding up the middle of a move.

Select whole track / Move 1 frame earlier / Move 1 frame later / Lock trackKey > Track, or right-click the limb > TrackShift+← Shift+→

Works on the picked limb's whole track, the row of all its keys.

  • Select whole track: picks every key of the limb.
  • Move 1 frame earlier / Move 1 frame later: slides the limb's whole track by a frame. In a looping clip the track wraps round the end, and frame 0 and the last frame get the same pose again. In a clip that does not loop, picked keys move on their own and the slide stops at frame 0 and at the clip's end.
  • Lock track (Unlock track when it is locked): a locked limb can't be picked or changed, and always plays its keys. Delete and moving leave its keys alone, and Select all skips them.

a head or an arm a few frames behind the body inside a walk or idle loop, protecting finished legs while working on the arms.

Share key times / Copy key times / Paste key times / Paste key times onlyKey > Key times and types

Makes limbs key on the same frames without changing their poses. Share key times: every picked limb gets a key on every frame any of them has one; nothing moves on those frames. Copy key times takes the picked limb's (or picked keys') frames; Paste key times keys the picked limb on them, posed as it already was. Paste key times only also removes its keys on other frames inside that stretch.

blocking with every limb keyed on the same frames, and one limb on another's beats.

Key / Breakdown / Generated, and Pick every key pose, breakdown or generated keyKey > Key times and types, or right-click a key > Key type

Sets what kind a picked key is, and picks all the keys of one kind. A Key (white diamond) is a pose you set. A Breakdown (blue) is a pose between two keys that shapes the move. Generated (small, grey) is a key Framez made from the motion.

  • Key, Breakdown, Generated: set the type of the picked keys.
  • Pick every key pose, Pick every breakdown, Pick every generated key: pick all keys of that type, on the picked limb or every limb.

The slider's Breakdown makes Breakdowns; bakes, Share key times, Paste key times and Get from Blender make Generated keys. With View > Playback steps > Keys only, playback holds on Key-type keys only. Types are kept with the Framez data, never in the game clip.

LoopsKey > LoopTab

Switches looping on or off for the clip: a looping clip plays round and round, in Framez's playback and in the game. Save to keep the setting.

Make it loopKey > Loop

Changes the clip so its end meets its start, and turns Loops on. It runs at once and its values open in Adjust.

  • Same pose at start and end: closes the loop by keying the last frame as frame 0.
  • Mirrored halves: builds the second half from the first, mirrored, for a walk.
  • Loop length: the length of the loop in frames.
  • Smooth the torso twist (mirrored halves only): eases the torso's twist through the middle.
  • Keep the head facing forward: keys the neck on every frame with the head facing straight ahead. It keeps frame 0's nod up or down and drops any turn or tilt to one side.

a walk, a run, an idle or a breath that must repeat without a jump.

Add layer / Add override layer / Key into / Picked layer / Merge into baseKey > Layers

Adds layers of keys over the clip. A change on a layer can be compared and removed without touching the clip's own keys. These are the animation layers as menu rows.

  • Add layer: a layer that is additive. Its poses are added on top of whatever plays under it.
  • Add override layer: a layer that replaces what is under it on the limbs it keys.
  • Key into: Base or one of the layers. New keys go there, and the timeline shows that layer's keys. It is greyed until a layer exists.
  • Picked layer (the row shows the layer's name): Additive, Override, Weight... (one weight for the whole layer, or a key at this frame), Key weight here (keys the weight at the playhead and adds the layer weight row), Body mask (the parts it plays on, see Edit mask), Mute or Unmute, Rename... and Delete layer.
  • Merge into base: bakes every layer into the clip's own keys.

changing a finished or imported clip, adding a lean or a breath over a run.

Watch onlyKey > Layers > Watch only

Mutes or solos parts of the rig, or plays another saved animation under this one, to watch a skill over the motion it will play on. It changes what Framez shows; saving is not affected.

  1. Choose Key > Layers > Watch only.
  2. Open Legs, Torso or Arms, head, weapon and pick Plays, Muted or Solo.
  3. Open Under and pick another saved animation of this rig, or Nothing.
  4. Open Under covers and pick the limbs that animation plays on.
  • Plays, Muted, Solo: Muted stops that part from playing; Solo plays only that part and the others show Muted.
  • Under: loops the animation you pick under this one, for example a run under a skill.
  • Under covers: Legs, torso plays the Under animation on the legs and torso; Unkeyed plays it on the limbs this clip has no keys on.

seeing a skill's arms over the run it will play on.

The Inspector has the same choices: see Watch only (mute, solo, Under).

Clip block for the rigKey > Layers > Clip block for (the rig's name)

Plays a saved clip as a block over your keys, starting on the playhead frame. The first block does not need the Scene workspace.

  1. Move the playhead to the frame where the block starts.
  2. Choose Key > Layers > Clip block for, then the rig's name.
  3. Pick one of the rig's other saves, one under From another rig (it plays by part name), or type an animation id or link in By id.

The block appears on a Clips row under the rig. See Clip blocks for moving, trimming, looping and masking it. The list says No other saves on this rig when there are none.

Check animationKey > Clean up, or View > Windows > Check animation

Lists what will look wrong in the game, each problem at its frame. Click a row to go to that frame with the limb picked. A pop or dead stop that several limbs share is one row naming them all. The count stays in the status bar; click it to check again.

  • Sliding feet: a foot on the floor that moves between frames.
  • Through the torso: an arm or leg going into the torso.
  • Pops and Dead stops: a pop is a limb end that jumps in speed in one frame; a dead stop is a limb that stands still between two moves. The Speed view shows both.
  • Overshoot: a curve shaped in the Curves window that goes past its keys. Back and Elastic easing overshoot on purpose and are not listed.
  • Rotation flips: a limb that flips half a turn in one frame.
  • Loop seam: a loop whose last frame does not match frame 0.
  • Frame 0: limbs that have keys but none on frame 0.
  • Priority: a clip saved at priority Core, the lowest, which the game's own walk and idle play over.
  • Faces: face keys with no Decal to show them, or a face image that will not load.

Each row has a button that fixes it as one undo step, and then the list checks again. The arrow beside a button opens that fix's numbers. Each fix is an item in Made: change its values, fade it, turn it off or delete it later.

  • Plant: holds a sliding foot.
  • Push out: turns the limb at its shoulder or hip until it leaves the torso.
  • Average: replaces a pop's frames with keys averaged from their neighbours.
  • Ease: slows the move into a dead stop and speeds the move out.
  • Clamp: sets an overshooting move to Smooth, which never passes its keys.
  • Unflip: turns a flipped limb back.
  • Close: keys the last frame as frame 0 and spreads the difference over the frames before it.
  • Key frame 0 and Set Action: key the missing frame 0, and set the clip's priority to Action.
  • Pick...: picks another face image when one will not load.
  • Fix all: runs every fix that needs no choice, as one undo step.

the last pass before saving or publishing.

Fixes change the base keys: with Animation layers, pick Base first.

Butterworth smooth / Gaussian smoothKey > Clean up, or the Curves window's Clean up

Smooths jitter out of the keys that are there; no key is added or removed. Butterworth keeps the big moves and takes the shake out. Gaussian softens everything evenly.

  1. Pick the limb, or the keys whose frames to smooth.
  2. Choose Key > Clean up > Butterworth smooth or Gaussian smooth. It runs at once and its values open in Adjust.
  3. Lower the Cut-off, or widen the Width, for smoother motion.
  • Filter: Butterworth or Gaussian.
  • Cut-off (Hz, Butterworth): lower is smoother. Order: 2 (gentle) or 4 (sharp).
  • Width (frames, Gaussian): wider is smoother.
  • Strength (%): how much of the smoothing is used.
  • Limbs (picked or all), the six channels (Rotation X, Y, Z, Position X, Y, Z) and the Frames From and To (the picked keys' by default).
  • Blend in and Blend out (frames): ease the smoothing in and out at the ends of the range.
  • Setups: the Setups row in Adjust lists Light, Heavy, Soft Gaussian, Capture clean-up.

after Pose from video, an import, or a clip from Blender or mocap.

Remove peaksKey > Clean up

A one-frame spike, far off the line between its neighbours, becomes their average. It works on the picked keys, or the picked limb, or every limb, and runs at once with its values in Adjust.

  • Peaks over (degrees) and Moves over (studs): a turn or a move further than this off the line between its neighbours is a peak.
  • Longest peak (1 to 8 frames): a run of up to this many frames off the line counts as one peak.
  • Channels, Limbs and Frames: what to check.
  • Setups: the Setups row in Adjust lists Capture pops, Single frames, Turns only.

single-frame jumps in a clip captured from video.

Euler filterKey > Clean up

Turns back a limb that flips half a turn in one frame, until it flips back; the limbs it carries keep their place. It runs at once and its values open in Adjust.

  • Flip over (degrees): a turn of more than this between two frames may be a flip.
  • Back under (degrees): it is a flip when a half turn about one of the limb's own axes brings the limb back to under this.
  • Limbs and Frames: only flips inside them are turned back.
  • Setups: the Setups row in Adjust lists Sensitive, Strict, Big flips only.

an imported or video clip where a forearm or a hand spins round for a frame.

Unbake at contactsKey > Clean up

Turns a key on every frame into a few key poses: where the feet land and lift, where a hand, foot, the head or the weapon tip stops or turns, and the frames that need keys until every frame is within the Error allowed. Every key is Smooth; turning points and added frames are Breakdowns. It runs at once and its values open in Adjust.

  • Error (degrees): the most a frame may be off after the keys are thinned.
  • Foot contacts (with Contact height and Slide under): keys where a foot lands and lifts.
  • Turning points (with Slow under, Turn over, Moving over): keys where a limb slows to a stop or turns.
  • Same frames: every limb keyed on the same frames. Off, each limb gets its own.
  • New keys: Smooth (auto clamped) or Linear.
  • Setups: the Setups row in Adjust lists Tight, Loose, Contacts only, Turns only.

changing a Blender or video clip by its poses instead of 60 keys a second.

Clean up captured motionKey > Clean up

Cleans up a clip captured from video or mocap: smooths the motion, puts the feet on the floor wherever one is planted (jumps keep their height), plants sliding feet and, if you choose In place, takes out the travel. One undo step. It runs at once and its values open in Adjust.

  • Smooth (0 to 100 %): 0 leaves the motion as it is, 100 smooths hardest. Order: 2 (gentle) or 4 (sharp).
  • Feet on floor: raises or lowers the body until a planted foot stands on the floor. Planted under (studs): a foot that moves less than this in a frame counts as planted. Sole within (studs): a planted foot this close to the lowest sole of that frame stands on the floor.
  • Lock feet: plants feet that slide.
  • Travel: Keep the travel or In place.
  • Body turn error (degrees) and Body move error (studs): the body's keys are thinned to play within these.
  • Setups: the Setups row in Adjust lists Light, Heavy, In place, Smooth only.

after Pose from video, or on any captured clip that floats, jitters or skates.

Clean-up chainKey > Clean up

Runs several clean-up tools one after another in one step, each with its own values.

  1. Choose Key > Clean up > Clean-up chain. It runs at once and its values open in Adjust.
  2. Click a step under Steps to open its values. Use the arrows on a step to run it sooner or later, the tick to switch it off and the close button to take it out.
  3. Click Add a step and pick a clean-up tool to add one at the end.
  • Limbs and Frames: go into every step that has them.
  • Setups: the Setups row in Adjust lists Capture clean-up, Polish and Game-ready. Your own chains save there too.

the same clean-up on every captured clip: remove peaks, smooth, then reduce keys.

Reduce keysKey > Clean up

Removes the keys a clip does not need: a key goes when the keys either side of it play within the error you allow. It runs at once and its values open in Adjust.

  • Reduce by: Error, or Share of the keys.
  • Turn error (degrees) and Move error (studs): how far the kept keys may play from the motion.
  • Keep (%, with Share of the keys): the share of the keys to keep. The ones that matter most stay.
  • Limbs and Frames: what to thin.
  • Setups: the Setups row in Adjust lists Fine, Coarse, Rough, Keep a quarter.

after Pose from video, or a clip from Blender or mocap.

ModifiersKey > Clean up > Modifiers, or the Curves window's Modifiers

Adds motion to the picked limb without adding keys: noise, a wiggle, repeats and more play on top of the keys, which stay as they are.

  1. Pick the limb.
  2. Choose Key > Clean up > Modifiers > Add a modifier, then a kind.
  3. Set its values in the box and confirm.
  4. Choose Modifiers and click a modifier's row to change it later.

Kinds in Add a modifier:

  • Noise: smooth random motion. Strength, Scale (frames between bumps), Phase, Depth (finer detail, up to 8), Offset, Lacunarity, Roughness.
  • Wiggle: a wave that can die out. Amplitude, Frequency, Decay, Start.
  • Function: Sine, Cosine, Tangent, Square root, Natural log or Normalised sine, with Amplitude, Phase multiplier, Phase offset and Value offset.
  • Generator: a polynomial of the frame, Expanded or Factorized, any order up to 10, with a row per coefficient.
  • Envelope: stretches the motion between Min and Max (around Reference) to each point's own Min and Max. Add a point at the playhead.
  • Limits: keeps the value between Lowest value and Highest value, and holds the time before or after a frame (Hold before, Hold after).
  • Cycles: plays the keyed stretch again before and after the keys: Repeat, Repeat with offset or Mirrored. Linear carries on the end's last slope. Cycles before and Cycles after set how many times (0 is endless), and then it holds.
  • Loop: Cycles that repeat both ways. Smooth seam eases the last frames into the start.
  • Stepped: holds the limb for Step frames at a time.

Every modifier has its channels, From and To frames with Blend in and out (Fade in and out), an Influence (0 to 200 %) with Influence over its frames, and a switch to turn it off. Noise, Wiggle, Function and Generator also have Blend: Add on top, Replace, Subtract or Multiply. Modifiers work in the order of the list. Number boxes take sums such as 2*pi, 90/4 or max(1, 3) + 2^2.

  • Copy and paste: Copy modifiers copies the picked limb's modifiers. Paste after theirs adds them to this limb and every limb with picked keys; Paste in place of theirs replaces what those limbs have. One undo step.
  • Turn on or off, Delete, Order (Move up, Move down): change the picked limb's list. A later Replace wins.
  • Save as my look: keeps a modifier's values under a name, for every place or In this place. Your looks sit beside each kind in Add a modifier.
  • Apply to keys: bakes the modifiers into keys.

an idle's drift, a tail or antenna that wiggles after a hit, a breath that loops, a shot on twos.

Saving writes the result into the game clip and keeps the modifiers for Framez. They open again as modifiers.
